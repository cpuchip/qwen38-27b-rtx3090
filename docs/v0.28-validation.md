## The connector arm per tier

The 0.27.1 engine-death recipe: the offloading connector, the pool pinned near the working set, and a side-load
of long prompts so the conversation's blocks leave the GPU between turns and come back from CPU. On 0.27.1 every
tier died in the scheduler within minutes; on the port:

| tier | pool | reload batches during the run | engine | mission |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| int4 + DFlash2 | 36,739 | 73 | alive, no assert | 12/18, every turn coherent (judgment misses, not repetition) |
| int8 + DFlash2 | 31,200 | 40 | alive, no assert | 15/18 |
| bf16 + DFlash2 | 20,264 | 21 | alive, no assert | 15/18 (its no-connector score) |
| KVarN + MTP | 57,079 | 14 | alive, no assert | **2/18**: coherent text, the model insists the tracking tool is not available to it (15/18 without the connector) |

Reading: the 0.27.1 engine death does not occur on the port on any tier, and int4, int8 and bf16 keep their
no-connector scores under 21 to 73 reload batches. KVarN does not: with MTP, fourteen reloads are enough for
the model to lose the tool definitions that live in the system prompt's blocks, a different shape from the
repetition collapse: coherent text with lost knowledge rather than a repeated token. Put beside the KVarN + DFlash2
collapse here, both KVarN failures on the port are blocks re-entering KVarN's staging pool from outside the
normal write path (a reload from CPU, or an aligned-down re-prefill over a shared prefix), and the other tiers,
which write through stock slot mappings, do not have the failure. For the offload goal this means: on the
port the CPU offload connector is usable with int4, int8 and bf16, and not with KVarN until KVarN's residency
lifecycle handles re-entering blocks.

### The instrument speaks: what turn two actually recomputes

The third diagnostic routed the wide continuation through KVarN's slow attention path (fp32 SDPA over gathered
history) with the lifecycle untouched. Not healed: 7/18, the same string from turn two, ten of twelve replies
cut at the cap. The attention consumer is exonerated. But the debug gate that rode with it fired 246 times,
and the first prefix-hit request of turn two reads, on this machine:

```
seq_len=1553 query_len=657 committed=896
blocks k=7..12 = physical 840..845, every one slot=None flush=False
```

The conversation is 1,553 tokens and the previous turn ended near 1,540, but the hit is honoured only to 896,
a scheduler-block boundary (neither the 128-token KVarN block nor the true hit). The 657-token remainder is
recomputed as one continuation, and every block it writes into is one KVarN has already flushed to permanent
int4 and released from its fp16 pool (`slot None`). On later turns the pattern repeats: committed stays at 896
or 1,344 while the sequence grows. The int4 tier writes the same rows through stock slot mappings and simply
overwrites them; KVarN has no staging slot for a block it considers finished, and where those rows land is
the poison site. Two threads follow from this: what KVarN does with a write into a slotless block (the fix),
and why the hit stops at 896 on the port when 0.27.1 hit at the true boundary (the cause). The int4 tier's
tool-loop capping is the same continuation seen without corruption: with prefix caching off, int4 answers
turn two directly and caps one turn instead of six; bf16 with MTP caps nine turns, so the capping does not need
DFlash2's drafter group, only the port's prefix-cache resume.

### Two fixes, not one (after round four of the code review)

> **Correction (08:45Z, see "The continuation reading falls" below):** the re-staging half is not the collapse's cause. The same cell with prefix caching off, where no continuation exists, collapses identically. The hit-precision regression stands as a port defect; it is no longer the explanation of the KVarN + DFlash2 collapse.

The debug tuples read as pre-allocation state: KVarN's build re-stages every block the continuation touches
into a fresh fp16 slot and the scatter writes there, so rows are not dropped. The candidate poison site moves
to the block the recompute only partly covers (the last one, rows 1,536 to 1,553 of 128), flushed later as a
whole tile with the reused slot's previous contents in its unwritten rows. The fourth diagnostic clears a
newly assigned slot on allocation and pairs a post-allocation line with the pre-allocation one; its two
cells (alone, and stacked on the slow-path routing) are queued on both machines.

Separately, and confirmed from both trees: the port resumes turn two at 896 because 896 is the scheduler
block (the least common multiple of KVarN's 128-token block and the 448-token Mamba page; the promoted
sliding-window drafter group is excluded from that multiple), and 0.28's coordinator falls back to that
alignment when a manager cannot take a fine-grained hit. The 0.27.1 fork resumed the same configuration at
1,536 through its own sliding-window handling in the runner patch, which the regenerated 0.28 runner patch
does not carry. So there are two fixes: KVarN's re-staging must be correct under any wide continuation (the
correctness half, KVarN only), and the port must restore hit precision for the promoted group (the
regression half, every tier: this is what makes the port's turn two cap the tool loop on bf16, int8 and int4
and re-prefill hundreds of tokens per turn).

## The fix: one hunk the regenerated runner patch dropped (09:10Z)

The other seat compared the two KVarN runner patches hunk by hunk instead of guessing a mechanism. Eight files, same on both, except one: the 0.27.1 patch touches `model_executor/layers/attention/attention.py` twice and the regenerated 0.28.0 patch once. The missing hunk is the one that, for KVarN cache dtypes on a hybrid model without skip layers, pads the drafter's sliding-window pages to the Mamba page (the fork's own comment: instead of wasting 16-token blocks inside 1.8 MB uniform pages). Without it the drafter's group carries a page and block size that do not line up with the rest, which is what prints the "Disabling fine-grained prefix-cache hits ... SlidingWindowManager" marker on every DFlash2 cell and never on MTP, and what makes the scheduler block the LCM of 128 and 448.

Restored against the port's installed file it is a seven-line insertion after the `skip_page_size_padded` line, and the attribute it reads, `cache_config.mamba_page_size_padded`, exists in 0.28.0 core. Two cells on the validated image with that patch applied at boot:

| cell | marker at boot | score | tool calls | replies capped / cut | turn two |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KVarN + DFlash2 k=7, hunk restored | 0 | **16/18** | 72 | 1 / 0 | "NO-GO on that request. The pre-launch PAD is void: it was co..." |
| same, replicate | 0 | **11/18** | 85 | 3 / 0 | "Flight, FIDO. I can't uplink the pre-launch PAD. It was comp..." |
| baseline (three runs, above) | 1 | 6-9/18 | 16-28 | 0 / 9-11 | "duct Register Register ..." |

The repetition collapse is gone: no reply hits the cap, turn two is the same decision the 0.27.1 fork makes, and the tool-call count is back where KVarN with MTP sits. What remains is the second regression, the tool-loop capping (three of twelve turns on the replicate), which the drafter-free bf16 rows already isolated to the port's prefix caching on this hybrid and which this hunk does not address.

**The storage path (code review, round seven).** Without the hunk, `shared_page` is `None` for the drafter's sliding-window spec, so it starts at a 16-token block with a 65,536-byte page. The port's block-promotion patch then notices that page does not divide the Mamba page (1,827,840 bytes) and promotes the drafter to a 544-token block with a 2,228,224-byte page, which becomes the new maximum page for every group. Unification pads the target KVarN spec up to that maximum, 400,384 bytes more per manager block. Core allocation and group ownership use the unified page, but KVarN derives its block stride from its own packed shape, seventeen 128-token tiles per 2,176-token manager block. The allocator therefore advances by the promoted page while KVarN's packed view and its flush lifecycle advance by seventeen tiles; block ownership and byte ownership diverge, and a later allocation or flush overwrites bytes the target still addresses as another block. With the hunk, the drafter's page equals the Mamba page from the start, it is excluded from the promotion candidates, the maximum never grows, and the two views stay identical. The boot-time coordinator warning is a symptom of the same geometry, not the mechanism. The hunk is gated on the KVarN cache dtype, so it is a no-op for the int4, int8 and bf16 tiers, and a deployment with real skip layers keeps its own shared page.

Why prefix caching off still collapsed under the mechanism reading above: the damage is in the group geometry itself, present in the configuration whether or not a hit is ever served. The continuation tuples were a true description of the port's coarsened hits, not of the collapse.

### A root candidate for the scatter, pre-registered (04:45Z, September 4)

The bare venv on the native box is identical to the digit across runs; containers scatter on both boxes. What a fresh container re-decides at every boot and a bare install remembers on disk is kernel selection: Triton's autotune cache and torch.compile's inductor cache live under the user's home. And vLLM, on 0.27.1 and 0.28.0 alike, defaults inductor's `combo_kernels` and `benchmark_combo_kernel` to true when the user leaves them unset (`config/compilation.py`), which means the kernel used for a fused op is chosen by timing it at compile. Timing is not repeatable; a different winner is a different reduction order; a different reduction order is a different greedy trajectory. Every container cell in this document also ran with `VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE=1`, and the stock-style cells had no persisted home, so this box re-timed at every boot by construction.

Predictions, written before the rows: (a) the fork's launcher with the compile cache enabled and the shared cache volume mounted, two boots, lands on identical tool-call totals; (b) with combo-kernel benchmarking off, three boots land on identical totals whether or not the cache is enabled; (c) if the poll loop is a property of some kernel winners and not others, the benchmarking-off arm's capped count is stable across boots, at whatever value it settles on; if the poll loop is independent of kernel choice, it keeps scattering. Seven cells are running here; the native box runs the bare and container halves with caches wiped and persisted.

Prediction (a) holds twice: with the cache persisted, two boots are identical, and the two pairs show the mechanism whole. Prediction (b) fails: with the benchmarking knob off and the cache still disabled, boots still differ. So the per-boot variable is not the combo-kernel benchmark; it is what the cache pins. vLLM redirects both inductor's cache and Triton's cache into its own cache directory (`compilation/compiler_interface.py`, the block that sets `TORCHINDUCTOR_CACHE_DIR` and `TRITON_CACHE_DIR`) and turns on Triton's autotune-result caching by default with the comment "without this, Triton re-runs autotuning on every process restart" (`env_override.py`). Triton's autotuner picks a kernel configuration by timing candidates, on purpose, for speed; the result is written to disk so the next boot reuses it. `VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE=1`, which every container cell in this document set, and a fresh container with no persisted home, both make every boot re-run that race. A different winner is a different tiling and reduction order, a slightly different float result, a different argmax on a near tie, a different greedy trajectory, and on this task a different chance of the poll loop. The native box's arm A shows the other half: within one boot, two missions are identical in all 124 calls; four boots of the same image were four different fixed points.

What this does and does not say. It says the scatter is per-boot kernel selection re-raced when the caches are absent, and that persisting vLLM's cache directory makes a container as deterministic as a bare install. It does not say which winners cause the poll loop or why this box's rate is higher; it does say the earlier environment reasoning (drivers, WSL2 alone) was chasing a consequence of the harness's own cache flag and of fresh containers. For the fork's users: mount a persistent `~/.cache/vllm` (or the launcher's `/cache`) into the container and leave `VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE` unset, and A/B rows become comparable across boots.

The first retracts this document's own capping item, which he had entered in the tracker as a live bug in main: the cap is this bench's per-turn budget rather than anything the serving stack imposes, it is not specific to 0.28, and the claim that turning prefix caching off removes it does not survive its own rows (74/1, 70/1, 25/0 and 106/3). The second is the autotune nondeterminism and the compile-cache rule, so the rule he drew from it has somewhere to point.

The third item is his, and it is the one open ask: on the reference 3090 the merged 0.28 accepts 3.19 tokens per verify step against 0.27.1's 2.66 with DFlash2, which is the same regression this document measured independently at 3.1 against 2.6. He asked for a bisect of the DFlash2 patch group rather than a guess at the 4,247 changed lines of the lookup patch. The census answers that without a boot: the group is unchanged hunk for hunk except in one file, where the speculator patch goes from 10 hunks to 15 and the model runner from 2 to 3. Three of the five new hunks change behaviour.

1. The drafter is asked for fewer tokens per step. The 0.28 form clamps `draft_block` to the checkpoint's trained block when the lookup is on, and `num_query_per_req` follows it, where 0.27.1 used the full speculative width. The remaining verify positions are filled from context instead of from the drafter, and the patch's own comment names `VLLM_DFLASH2_LOOKUP=0` as the honest control.
2. The selector walk lost its float64 path. The cast that read `tl.float64 if USE_FP64 else tl.float32` is now `tl.float32`, while the flag stays declared and passed, so the knob looks live and no longer selects that cast.
3. The proposal distribution is truncated to the request's top-k and top-p, disabled by `VLLM_DFLASH2_DRAFT_TOPK_TOPP=0`, and it cannot explain a loss at temperature 0 because it never runs there.

Incidental from the same census: three patches on merged main carry hunks against `.orig` files, which are the backup files `patch` writes, so the series was regenerated from a tree that still had them. Inert at runtime, and the fingerprint of the regeneration that dropped the page-padding hunk in the first place.

### The draw test, and the correction it forced (15:10Z, September 4)

The pin cycle froze two trees and forced six pins, which is n=2 on the thing that matters. Eight fresh draws are running with the prediction registered in advance: two warps on the block-inverse merge kernel gives the short loop, four gives the long one. The first two draws answer it.

| draw | merge kernel | K-transpose-K kernel | recompute-W-U kernel | trajectory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 warps, 2 stages | 8 warps, 3 stages, BK 64 | 8 warps, 3 stages | 160 calls, 7 capped |
| 2 | 2 warps, 2 stages | 4 warps, 4 stages, BK 32 | 4 warps, 4 stages | 188 calls, 9 capped |

The prediction is wrong twice over. Two warps gave the long loop both times, and the same merge configuration gave two different trajectories. The kernels that moved are the two the cycle reported as inert, so their inertness was a property of the trees the cycle froze rather than of the kernels. The honest claim is the weaker one: the winner set as a whole selects the trajectory, and any member of it can move the result. The merge kernel is decisive between the two particular draws the cycle compared, which is a real finding about those draws and not a general one.

This is the value of registering a prediction and then drawing rather than pinning. The strong claim went out on the pull request thread and in the autotune issue, and it needs correcting there once the remaining draws are in.

### The acceptance regression (#73): three mechanisms proposed, three retracted (15:30Z, September 4)

The maintainer asked for a bisect of the DFlash2 patch group rather than a guess at the 4,247 changed lines of the lookup patch. The census answered without a boot and answered wrongly, three times.

**The method was wrong first.** Comparing the patch files hunk by hunk flags a hunk that merely grew or moved its boundaries as new behaviour. The right comparison is the installed files. Pulling both speculators out of the two built images gives 86 changed lines in one file, and says that `dflash2/lookup.py` is byte-identical between the trees, which the patch-level reading never revealed.

**Then the conditions retired what was left.** The draft-block clamp turned out to be present in the 0.27.1 tree too, at the same lines, which a 0.27.1 boot confirms by logging the same clamped draft width. And every remaining difference is a division by temperature: the cached proposal row, the truncation site, and a greedy-only tie-break change. The checkpoint's configuration sets temperature 1.0, the maintainer's decode guard passes no temperature flag, and a boot here logs the server resolving to 1.0. At exactly 1.0, dividing by temperature is the identity, so none of them can act in the regime the number was measured in.

**The control, run rather than argued.** The one-line restoration of the temperature-scaled proposal row, against an unedited arm, one frozen kernel draw, the maintainer's own decode guard:

| arm | tokens per step |
|---|---|
| as merged | 2.78 |
| proposal row restored | 2.77 |

Identical, as the arithmetic requires. The same pair also shows why this box cannot publish absolute throughput: prefill moved from 3,303 to 705 tokens per second between the two arms, and the edit sits in the draft selector where it cannot touch prefill at all, so that difference is the box rather than the change. The ratio within a run survived what the absolute numbers did not.

**What survives, and where it points.** The premise the 0.28 code states twice, at the caching site and again in the draft-logits spec, is that the rejection sampler divides cached draft logits by temperature itself. An independent read of the installed sampler on the other box found it applies temperature to the target side only and leaves the draft side untouched. That inconsistency is real and inert at temperature 0 or 1, and would bite at any other value.

The open question is now a metric question rather than a performance one. Tokens per step is derived as one plus accepted over drafts from two engine counters, and the two arms are different engine versions, so the ruler is part of the delta rather than a fixed measure across it. At the tested width the drafter proposes seven tokens while the lookup fills eight. The check needs no cross-tree comparison at all: drafted tokens divided by drafts, within a single arm, says whether the lookup-filled positions are counted as drafts.

One instrument result worth carrying. The teacher-forced acceptance bench keys its cached target on the prompt alone, with no build, width, or boot in the key, so replaying one configuration's target under another is expected to go off trajectory whenever the batch shape changes, which the bench's own header documents. Its clean-chunk fraction is the validity guard, and a tokens-per-step figure computed over a handful of surviving chunks is not a measurement. That instrument is invalid across builds whose text diverges; the maintainer's own decode guard is not, because there each build generates its own continuation and scores its own drafter against its own target.

### The acceptance regression, found: an unset launcher field (16:10Z, September 4)

The fourth mechanism is the one with a confirming arm, and the evidence that matters needs no patch at all.

**The config fact.** On 0.27.1 the fork's own speculator allocated the draft-logits buffer unconditionally, with a comment stating why: its selector samples probabilistically for non-greedy requests, so rejection sampling always needs the realized proposal distribution. The 0.28 port dropped that unconditional allocation and inherited the upstream base class, which allocates only when the speculative config asks for probabilistic draft sampling. Upstream's default is greedy, documented as treating the draft probabilities as one-hot. The launcher sets that field explicitly for the other speculator and omits it for this one, so the merged tree silently runs the other behaviour. Both boot logs say so outright, reporting the buffer present on one tree and absent on the other.

**The mechanism.** With no proposal distribution the rejection kernel treats the draft probability as one, and the acceptance test collapses from the ratio of target to draft down to the bare target probability. Since the draft probability is at most one, the ratio test accepts at least as often, so the merged path is strictly stricter. That predicts the direction, which the first reading of the change did not.

**The control that needs no edit.** Running the sibling speculator over the same two builds, same box, same hour, same workload:

| path | launcher sets the field | acceptance change across the two builds |
|---|---|---|
| the other speculator | yes | plus 6.5% |
| this one | no | minus 10.5% |

If the port carried a general acceptance regression, both would show it.

**The counters, three boxes.** Round size is unchanged everywhere, so this is proposal survival rather than block length.

| box | 0.27.1 | merged | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| maintainer's, tokens per step | 3.19 | 2.66 | minus 16.6% |
| this box, tokens per step | 3.176 | 2.621 | minus 17.5% |
| native 3090, tokens per step | 3.751 | 3.457 | minus 7.8% |

**The fix arm.** Setting the field recovers most or all of the gap. On the native box acceptance per drafted token goes from 34.4% to 36.8% against a 38.4% reference. On this box two runs give 32.1% and 30.2% against a 30.3% reference, so the recovery reaches parity rather than exceeding it; the first run alone suggested an overshoot and the repeat retired that reading.

**What the repeats mean, and they mean different things per box.** The native box seeds from zero and holds its kernels fixed, so its repeats came back bit identical on every counter and are replays rather than samples. This box holds the seed too but re-races the autotuner every boot, so its repeats vary the kernel axis: two shipped runs agree within one percent relative, while two runs with the field set differ by 5.6%. That spread difference is itself consistent with the mechanism, since the shipped path selects proposals deterministically and the fixed path samples them. So the kernel axis is bounded here and the seed axis is bounded nowhere yet, which is what the other box's seed sweep is for.

**The residual, stated plainly.** The field does not explain everything on the native box, and the untested candidate for the remainder is the emitted-token accounting patch the maintainer bisected himself as worth about a quarter of the gap, which feeds the block-length policy and is the natural explanation for the small round-size drift on the fixed arm.

**A caution worth carrying.** Five separate candidates in this component were retired by the same argument: nearly every difference in it is a question of where a division by temperature happens, and the measured regime runs at exactly one, where that division is the identity in every floating-point format. A diff cannot tell you whether a change can act in the regime the number came from.

### The cohort result, stratified: the fix works only where the drafter carries the block (16:30Z, September 4)

Four seeds by eight prompts at 1024 output tokens, paired by seed and prompt, tokens per step as the primary metric, split on the shipped block size so the stratifier is a pre-treatment variable. The threshold was registered before the data.

| stratum | cells | shipped | field set | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| overall | 32 | 3.440 | 3.730 | 2.77 |
| drafted tokens per round = 7 | 18 | 2.886 | 3.425 | 4.22 |
| drafted tokens per round > 7 | 14 | 4.152 | 4.123 | -0.22 |

So the overall effect is suggestive rather than established, and the stratification is the finding. Positions filled from context never came from the drafter, so they carry no proposal distribution to normalise against in either configuration, and the field's effect dilutes to nothing where the lookup is engaged. That reading was registered in advance as the interesting outcome rather than found afterwards, and the same direction appears on the other box at a quarter of the sample size.

The earlier short-prompt figure of plus 0.48 tokens per step is therefore a stratum-specific result, since every row of that cell had the lookup inert. The overall figure is also a mixture whose value depends on what fraction of a workload sits in each stratum, so it is a property of the cohort rather than a constant of the fix.

Two ordering constraints came out of the other box's replication. The degeneracy guard must run before any stratification on block size, because a collapsed generation makes the lookup engage and therefore sorts into the engaged stratum by construction, where a single such row moved their stratum estimate by two tokens per step and flipped their overall sign. And per-request figures are comparable only within a fixed request order, because the long block is sticky and coasts on prior state without consulting the emitted-token count.

The report drafted for the issue is at `docs/drafts/issue-73-report.draft.md`.

### What the pieces are, for the reader who wants the mechanism and not only the numbers (September 5)

**The six autotuned kernels.** All of them belong to the chunked Gated DeltaNet path, the linear-attention layers this model uses in place of most of its softmax attention. Each processes the sequence in chunks of 64 tokens, and the recurrent state is carried between chunks. Triton autotunes a kernel by timing a small grid of launch configurations at first use, warps per block and pipeline stages, and keeping the fastest. `chunk_local_cumsum_scalar` computes the running sum of the gate values inside a chunk. `chunk_scaled_dot_kkt_fwd` forms the key-times-key-transpose product that the delta rule needs. `merge_16x16_to_64x64_inverse` builds the inverse of a 64 by 64 lower-triangular matrix from four 16 by 16 inverses, which is the solve that turns the delta rule into a chunked recurrence. `recompute_w_u_fwd` recomputes two intermediate tensors from that inverse. `chunk_gated_delta_rule_fwd_h` carries the state across chunks. `chunk_fwd_kernel_o` produces the layer output. Two of the six never changed configuration across eight cold boots here, four took three or four different winners each, and no two boots agreed on the full set. A different winner is a different partition of the same tiles, therefore a different order of floating-point accumulation, therefore a different last bit, and both warp counts of the merge kernel sit at the same distance from a float64 reference, so this is rounding order and not accuracy.

**Why one bit moves a conversation.** At temperature zero the next token is the argmax. Two candidates that the model ranks within a rounding error of each other are decided by that last bit, and once the conversation takes the other branch it never rejoins. The twelve-turn tool loop has such a near tie early, which is why one image gave 58 to 189 tool calls across boots. At the model's recommended sampling the choice at that position is a draw from a distribution rather than a comparison of two nearly equal numbers, so the same last-bit difference changes the odds of a branch by a negligible amount instead of deciding it.

**The field.** `draft_sample_method` tells the speculator whether the drafter picks each proposed token by argmax or by sampling, and, downstream of that, whether the rejection sampler receives the drafter's probability for each proposal. With the field unset the base class allocates no buffer for those probabilities, the selector is handed temperature zero and picks by argmax, and the rejection kernel treats every draft probability as one. The acceptance test in that kernel accepts a proposal when a uniform draw falls under the target probability divided by the draft probability. With the draft probability pinned to one that ratio can only be smaller, so fewer proposals survive. The 0.27.1 fork allocated the buffer unconditionally, which is why the field's absence cost nothing there and cost ten to seventeen percent of tokens per step on 0.28.

**The block-length policy.** With the lookup on, the drafter is asked for the block the checkpoint was trained for, seven tokens, and the engine verifies eight positions. The remaining positions up to the configured width of fifteen are filled from the request's own context by a suffix lookup, but only while a request is reproducing its context, because a long block costs attention on every step whether or not the tail is accepted. The policy that decides this runs on the host once per step, one step stale by design so that it never synchronises the device. It enters the long block only after two qualifying steps in a row, where a qualifying step is one where the lookup had something to put in the tail and the step that just finished emitted a full block. Once entered, `VLLM_DFLASH2_LOOKUP_STICKY` steps of coasting follow, default three, during which the policy consults nothing. That coast is what makes per-request drafted-token counts depend on the previous request.

**The emitted count and the contradicting comment.** The qualifying test reads `last_num_emitted`, which the speculator writes from the sampler's counters on every step. `input_batch.py` defines the rejected count as the number of logits minus the number sampled, on both engines, so the sampled count was always the number of tokens the sampler emitted. The 0.27.1 formula subtracted the rejected count from it and therefore removed every rejection twice. The 0.28 patch corrected the formula; the comment inherited from 0.27.1 described the old meaning and was left standing. Reversing the patch feeds the policy a count that is too low, so it qualifies less often and spends less time in the long block. On the maintainer's cohort that scored higher. Whether that is because the long block was not paying for itself there is exactly what the sticky arm below measures with an honest knob instead of a wrong number.

### The policy knob, measured: nothing left to recover on the production configuration (September 5)

The pull request body claimed the maintainer's 0.13 tokens per step from reversing the emitted-count patch was long-block occupancy reached through a wrong number, and that the policy's own knob would reproduce it honestly. Three arms on the cohort, field set in all three, paired, guard applied first and removing nothing:

| arm | tokens per step | drafted tokens per round |
|---|---|---|
| shipped patch, coast 3 | 3.735 | 7.140 |
| patch reversed, coast 3 | 3.739 | 7.113 |
| shipped patch, coast 0 | 3.737 | 7.055 |

Both knobs lowered occupancy as predicted and neither moved throughput, t of 0.13 and 0.10 against a registered bar of 3, null inside both strata. So the honest knob does what it says to block occupancy and there is nothing on this configuration for it to recover. The likeliest reason is the regime: his measurement was taken with the field unset, where argmax drafting keeps acceptance lower and the emitted count nearer the policy's threshold, so the two formulas disagree more often. With the field set, more steps clear the threshold under either formula and the difference stops mattering, which matches the one-in-ninety-nine disagreement the other box instrumented. The comment fix stands on the definitions alone; the claim that the knob recovers his gain is withdrawn as measured, on the pull request.

### The order effect, mechanism settled (September 5, 02:45Z)

Three arms on this box, six seeds in two orders each, one boot per arm. With an ordinary prompt no request entered the long block and every counter was order-invariant, which is a check that could not fail rather than a null. With a prompt that makes the model reproduce its context the long block engaged on every seed and request order changed drafts and accepted tokens on four of six seeds, one seed from 31 verify rounds to 112, so on this box the effect moves the trajectory itself. With prefix caching genuinely off, confirmed as `enable_prefix_caching=False` in the server log and by the rows differing from the cache-on arm, the effect persisted on four of six seeds. So the driver is the block-length policy coasting into the next request, and prefix caching is ruled out here.

The first cache-off arm was a no-op: the launcher's flag adds engine arguments only at one, and at zero the engine default for a generative hybrid model resolves prefix caching to on, so both arms ran identically and the bit-identical result was forced. The rerun passed the engine's own off switch and printed its resolved setting before its numbers. That rule now stands for every arm in this document: print the engine's resolved configuration, then the number.

## Arc two: throughput. Registrations before numbers (September 6)

**Arm 1, draft width 3 to 7, per-position acceptance and wall clock.** Registered: the per-position curve decreases steeply; the wall-clock optimum sits below seven if positions five to seven accept under ten percent. Falsifier: decode rate monotone in width up to seven means shorter buys nothing. Before arm 1 reported, the other box's arm-3 cell supplied the curve on the same cohort at production sampling: 80.1 percent at position zero falling to 19.9 at position six, so no drafter position is under ten percent and the premise is false on acceptance grounds. The wall-clock half is what arm 1 still answers.

**The finding neither box registered.** On that same cell, positions zero to six returned 98.8 percent of all accepted tokens and the eight lookup-filled positions returned 1.2 percent, with a thirty-fold drop between position six and position seven. Two readings the cell cannot separate: the long block is rarely entered so those positions rarely exist, or they exist and accept near zero. The counter is cumulative over rounds in which a position existed, so the raw counts are deflated by the first and the two look identical.

**Arm 2, the cheap-context threshold at 0, 4096, 8192 and 16384, is the discriminator, and it now carries two competing hypotheses.** H1, from the source comment: forcing the long block below the threshold wins, eight percent at the C1 cohort, because extra verify positions are cheap at short context. H2, from the curve: forcing it loses, because positions seven to fourteen accept near zero even when they always exist, so the extra positions cost attention and return nothing, and the loss grows with the threshold. The discriminating number is the per-position accept rate at seven to fourteen under the highest threshold, where the long block is unconditional on this cohort. Under two percent is H2 and the lookup is not earning its width on ordinary text; tens of percent is H1 back in play and the lookup was starved rather than wrong. No sign is predicted. The client records per-position deltas on every row, so the cell needs no change.

**Arm 3, drafter truncation under real sampling, on the other box.** Five cells, field set throughout, the rank half of the mechanism made live by matched cells at top-k eight, sign unpredicted, effect registered under 0.15 tokens per step. Halted after an independent review found that a null with no measured truncation activation is not an experiment, and that a fixed request order does not give equal state when truncation changes the outputs that feed the suffix lookup. Redesign to be registered before further boots. The rule that came out of it: measure that the mechanism fired, not only that the switch was set.

### A box fact that reframes every card-1 clock in this document (September 6)

`nvidia-smi` reports this box's card 1 negotiating PCIe Gen4 at 4 lanes, with 16 available, while card 0 negotiates 8 at idle. Under an identical configuration card 0 decodes at about 162 tokens per second and card 1 at about 39, and card 1 does so at 99 percent utilisation drawing about 155 watts in the P2 state, which is a GPU waiting on its host rather than computing. With WSL2 pinned memory and prefix caching on, every decode step crosses that link.

So every wall-clock figure this document reports from card 1, including the prefill collapse between the two proposal-row arms and the low counter-cell rates, is a four-lane artefact layered on WSL2 and not a property of the change under test. Counter-based ratios from card 1, tokens per step, acceptance and per-position acceptance, are unaffected and were the ones published. Arm 1 runs entirely on card 0 and its decode rates are trustworthy within the card. Arm 2 runs entirely on card 1: its registered discriminator is counter-based and stands; its decode-rate reading is being repeated on card 0 for the two end cells. The other box remains the only one whose absolute rates compare to the maintainer's.
