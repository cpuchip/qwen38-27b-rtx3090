Three DFlash fixes merged upstream after the 0.28.0 tag, and one of them sits under the field you just set in production. Verified against the merged PRs and against the built 0.28.0 tree, not from a summary.

**1. `draft_sample_method: "probabilistic"` on 0.28.0 is faster and not lossless.** vllm-project/vllm#54282, merged 2026-08-29 as `fe755c889`, three days after the tag. Under probabilistic drafting the Gumbel noise used to re-sample a rejected draft token is drawn from the same Philox offset as the noise that produced the draft, so the residual under-weights exactly the high-probability tokens that lost the draft argmax, and, in the PR's words, the output distribution is no longer the target's. Greedy drafting is unaffected, because with no draft logits the draft never samples. The fix is a salt on the draft's Philox offset; I grepped the built tree and it is absent. So the field recovers your 16 percent and, until that commit is in the build, trades the README's losslessness claim for it. Cherry-picking `fe755c889` is the clean fix and should apply, since it touches the draft's noise stream and nothing the fork patches.

**2. `CTX=long` and `CTX=huge` run the DFlash drafter on a wrong attention schedule.** vllm-project/vllm#54374, merged 2026-09-04 as `5093e4844`. FlashAttention's ahead-of-time split schedule is computed once for one sliding-window configuration, and it stays on when the drafter is the only FlashAttention consumer, which is the case whenever the target runs another backend. Your `CTX=fast` target is FlashAttention, so two configurations turn the schedule off and the drafter is, in the PR author's phrase, correct by coincidence. `CTX=long` is FlashInfer and `CTX=huge` is Triton attention, so both leave it on. The PR's symptom is acceptance collapsing to 1.0 under full CUDA graphs, with an illegal memory access in some configurations. Your README documents that symptom and attributes it elsewhere.

**3. The drafter's RoPE layout is taken from the target.** vllm-project/vllm#54373, merged 2026-08-31 as `d61b6e187`. The loader stamps the target's `is_neox_style` onto the draft config, which is only right when the two agree; on one model where they do not, acceptance was 1.03 against 7.78. Whether the Qwen3.8 drafter agrees with its target I have not checked; it is one line in the draft's config.

**One more, in the tree already and switched off.** `rejection_sample_method: "block"` is a valid value at 0.28.0 and block verification is wired into the V2 rejection sampler that DFlash2 uses. It is Sun et al.'s block verification: non-greedy only, lossless, provably never worse than token-by-token, with 5 to 8 percent reported. One config key. I am measuring it on the cohort now and will bring numbers rather than the claim.

Two more from the same sweep are relevant to #75: upstream commits `#54794` and `#53955` touch the compile-cache and autotune behaviour that issue describes. I have not read those two beyond their titles.

DRAFT ADDITIONS (2026-09-05, arc two; for Michael's pen, not posted). Each item is final unless marked pending; numbers and conditions are in docs/v0.28-validation.md from the "Arc two: throughput" heading, with the fresh-eyes review beside it.

**5. Block verification, measured.** `rejection_sample_method: "block"` is wired on the V2 rejection sampler this fork runs (rejection_sampler.py:96-97 in the built tree) and it is live: against standard rejection it changes acceptance decisions on every row. It is also worth about nothing on the realistic cohort: two boot pairs at DFLASH_TOKENS=15, +0.006 and +0.022 tokens per step, each within one row standard error of zero, step cost identical to 1 percent. The paper's 5 to 8 percent is excluded at 3.6 to 5.8 standard errors with the mechanism demonstrably engaged. At the default width 7 the same: +0.024 tokens per step (18 of 32 rows positive, standard error 0.054), 5 percent sits 3.1 standard errors above. Live and worth nothing at both widths; correctly off.

**6. The lookup's first position, a seam worth a look.** With the long block forced (LOOKUP_CHEAP_CTX above the context length) so positions 7 to 14 are always verified, the acceptance rate conditional on reaching the position is 69 to 79 percent at positions 0 to 6, 5 to 6 percent at position 7, and 70 to 96 percent at positions 8 to 14, on both cards here. The lookup almost never gets its first token right and is as good as the drafter once it has; the tail is worthless because it is gated behind that one position. That shape is what a boundary or off-by-one at the drafter-to-lookup seam would produce, and it is cheap to probe in the lookup patch. Whether it is a bug or just what a suffix match looks like on non-repeating text, we have not determined; the cohort accepts 1.2 to 1.6 percent of its tokens from those positions either way.

**7. MTP at matched width, for the README's framing.** MTP can run at width 7 on this checkpoint (`DRAFT_TOKENS=7 SPEC=mtp`; `DFLASH_TOKENS` is only read on the dflash2 branch). At matched width 7, DFlash2 accepts 15.9 percent more per step than MTP and is 8.3 percent cheaper per step; the two drafters are identical at position 0 and MTP has lost 40 percent of DFlash2's conditional rate by position 6. Widening MTP from 4 to 7 nets slightly worse throughput (+13.4 percent accepted per step for +11.1 percent step cost). The edge is the drafter's depth, not width and not the card. Native 3090, one boot per configuration, boots replay on that box.

**8. One line for the width discussion.** The launcher comment says losing async scheduling at batch 1 costs under 1 percent. Measured directly at width 7 with only the scheduler changed: about zero on a 4090 under WSL2, 2.07 percent of throughput on a native 3090. Right on one box, low by two on the other; not a note on its own.

**9. Reproduction mode's price under WSL2.** Pending: DFLASH_TOKENS=15 costs 2.4x per step on a 4090 under WSL2 (56 against 23 ms, three boots to 1 percent) and 0.3 percent on the native 3090 with scheduling pinned. Synchronous scheduling is excluded as the cause (width 7 with it off costs the same as with it on, here), and so is the lookup as a switch (LOOKUP=0 at width 15 crashes the engine core, as your issue 73 incidentals already say; we read that line late). The cells that remain (split-KV verify attention off at 15, the Triton race pinned at 15, width 8, async on at 15 with the KV pin raised) are running; the note gets the mechanism, not the number, when they land. Two side findings for the same paragraph: async scheduling on at width 15 (which needs the KV pin raised about 10 MiB, the only reason the launcher's rule looked like a refusal) costs 9.1 percent of throughput on the native 3090 because padding every step to 15 bypasses the adaptive length, so the launcher's coupling of synchronous scheduling to the long block is a saving and its comment undersells the reason; and losing async at width 7 costs about zero on a 4090 under WSL2 and 2.07 percent on the native 3090.

**Not for the note, recorded for us.** The boot-to-boot divergence on this box is a binary state (four boots of one configuration form exactly two trajectories, differing on the same ten rows in rounds and accepted tokens), present on both cards of this WSL2 host and absent on the native 3090 across eight boots; the Triton autotune race is excluded as the source (force-first-config boots diverge more, not less, and the first-listed config runs twice as slow at width 7 on a 4090, so VLLM_TRITON_FORCE_FIRST_CONFIG is neither a determinism fix nor a speed-up here), and card, driver, WSL2 and container are confounded between the boxes. The cross-box difference in acceptance level is inside one box's own sampling spread and is not a finding; the greedy pair with completion text saved is the only clean comparison. Issue 75's scatter needs a different explanation than the one we gave it; do not reopen that thread until the cache pair, the trajectory count and the greedy pair land.
