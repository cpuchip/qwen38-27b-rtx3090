Three DFlash fixes merged upstream after the 0.28.0 tag, and one of them sits under the field you just set in production. Verified against the merged PRs and against the built 0.28.0 tree, not from a summary.

**1. `draft_sample_method: "probabilistic"` on 0.28.0 is faster and not lossless.** vllm-project/vllm#54282, merged 2026-08-29 as `fe755c889`, three days after the tag. Under probabilistic drafting the Gumbel noise used to re-sample a rejected draft token is drawn from the same Philox offset as the noise that produced the draft, so the residual under-weights exactly the high-probability tokens that lost the draft argmax, and, in the PR's words, the output distribution is no longer the target's. Greedy drafting is unaffected, because with no draft logits the draft never samples. The fix is a salt on the draft's Philox offset; I grepped the built tree and it is absent. So the field recovers your 16 percent and, until that commit is in the build, trades the README's losslessness claim for it. Cherry-picking `fe755c889` is the clean fix and should apply, since it touches the draft's noise stream and nothing the fork patches.

**2. `CTX=long` and `CTX=huge` run the DFlash drafter on a wrong attention schedule.** vllm-project/vllm#54374, merged 2026-09-04 as `5093e4844`. FlashAttention's ahead-of-time split schedule is computed once for one sliding-window configuration, and it stays on when the drafter is the only FlashAttention consumer, which is the case whenever the target runs another backend. Your `CTX=fast` target is FlashAttention, so two configurations turn the schedule off and the drafter is, in the PR author's phrase, correct by coincidence. `CTX=long` is FlashInfer and `CTX=huge` is Triton attention, so both leave it on. The PR's symptom is acceptance collapsing to 1.0 under full CUDA graphs, with an illegal memory access in some configurations. Your README documents that symptom and attributes it elsewhere.

**3. The drafter's RoPE layout is taken from the target.** vllm-project/vllm#54373, merged 2026-08-31 as `d61b6e187`. The loader stamps the target's `is_neox_style` onto the draft config, which is only right when the two agree; on one model where they do not, acceptance was 1.03 against 7.78. Whether the Qwen3.8 drafter agrees with its target I have not checked; it is one line in the draft's config.

**One more, in the tree already and switched off.** `rejection_sample_method: "block"` is a valid value at 0.28.0 and block verification is wired into the V2 rejection sampler that DFlash2 uses. It is Sun et al.'s block verification: non-greedy only, lossless, provably never worse than token-by-token, with 5 to 8 percent reported. One config key. I am measuring it on the cohort now and will bring numbers rather than the claim.

Two more from the same sweep are relevant to #75: upstream commits `#54794` and `#53955` touch the compile-cache and autotune behaviour that issue describes. I have not read those two beyond their titles.

DRAFT ADDITIONS (2026-09-05, arc two; for Michael's pen, not posted). Each item is final unless marked pending; numbers and conditions are in docs/v0.28-validation.md from the "Arc two: throughput" heading, with the fresh-eyes review beside it.

**5. Block verification, measured.** `rejection_sample_method: "block"` is wired on the V2 rejection sampler this fork runs (rejection_sampler.py:96-97 in the built tree) and it is live: against standard rejection every one of 32 rows contains at least one changed acceptance decision. It is also worth about nothing on the realistic cohort: two boot pairs at DFLASH_TOKENS=15, +0.006 and +0.022 tokens per step, each within one row standard error of zero, step cost identical to 1 percent. The paper's 5 to 8 percent is excluded at 3.6 to 5.8 standard errors with the mechanism demonstrably engaged. At the default width 7 the same: +0.024 tokens per step (18 of 32 rows positive, standard error 0.054), 5 percent sits 3.1 standard errors above. The cells exclude the reported 5 to 8 percent at both widths, but do not resolve a 1 to 2 percent gain; width 15 has two boot pairs and width 7 one.

**6. The lookup's first position, a seam worth a look.** With the long block forced (LOOKUP_CHEAP_CTX above the context length) so positions 7 to 14 are always verified, the acceptance rate conditional on reaching the position is 69 to 79 percent at positions 0 to 6, 5 to 6 percent at position 7, and 70 to 96 percent at positions 8 to 14, on both 4090s. The lookup almost never gets its first token right and is as good as the drafter once it has; the tail is worthless because it is gated behind that one position. That shape is consistent with either a seam defect or the ordinary gating of a suffix match, and the cell does not distinguish them; it is cheap to probe in the lookup patch. Whether it is a bug or just what a suffix match looks like on non-repeating text, we have not determined; the cohort accepts 1.2 to 1.6 percent of its tokens from those positions either way.

**7. MTP at matched width, for the README's framing.** MTP can run at width 7 on this checkpoint (`DRAFT_TOKENS=7 SPEC=mtp`; `DFLASH_TOKENS` is only read on the dflash2 branch). At matched width 7, DFlash2 accepts 15.9 percent more per step than MTP and is 8.3 percent cheaper per step; the two drafters are indistinguishable at position 0 (78.9 against 79.0 percent) and MTP has lost 40 percent of DFlash2's conditional rate by position 6. Widening MTP from 4 to 7 nets slightly worse throughput (+13.4 percent accepted per step for +11.1 percent step cost). On this cohort in one native-3090 boot per arm, matching width leaves a depth-growing DFlash2 advantage, so width alone does not explain the edge.

**8. One line for the width discussion.** The launcher comment says losing async scheduling at batch 1 costs under 1 percent. At width 7, one fast-side comparison found no measurable scheduling cost on the 4090 under WSL2, while the native engine-confirmed pair lost 2.07 percent; a second local async-off boot landed on the unrelated 45 ms slow observation. Right on one box, low by two on the other; not a note on its own.

**9. Reproduction mode's step cost on a 4090 is the split-KV verify attention, and its query maximum is the configuration variable.** DFLASH_TOKENS=15 costs 2.4x per step on a 4090 under WSL2 (56.5 against 23 ms, five boots) and 2 percent on the native 3090. A matched width-7 cell changed only VLLM_SPEC_DECODE_ATTN_QMAX from 8 to 16 and landed at 63.5 against 23.2 ms with all 32 rows identical on the recorded counters, which establishes a QMAX-dependent specialization penalty on this host: the kernel (spec_decode_attn.py) is plain @triton.jit, plans each step from the actual query length, and takes QMAX as a buffer dimension and a compile-time constant; why the specialization is slow is not measured. A maximum of 9 and of 12 run at 23.2 and 23.4, so the step is somewhere between 13 and 16. On the 3090 the same pair reads 25.0 against 25.1. Separately, SPEC_ATTN=0 at width 15 (stock attention serving the verify) runs at 23.6 ms per step on two boots, both, with a similar aggregate yield to the kernel-on boots (3.732 against 3.72 to 3.74 accepted tokens per step among the active rows), and both boots return a one-token completion on prompt 5 seed 2 where every kernel-on boot runs to 1024, the greedy answer is full, and the same switch at width 7 leaves the seed intact (one boot on the 4090, and four seeds both ways on the native 3090); the same seed empties under the forced Triton configuration at width 7, so this is a boundary seed that two of the three numerics changes tested moved, not a defect of the switch, and at width 15 the workaround is not output-neutral on this cohort; one more practical line beside it: width 15 at the launcher's defaults sits about 10 MiB from the KV-cache edge on a 24 GiB card (two boots on the 4090 came up, one attempt at the same settings on the 3090 failed with a hard ValueError, estimated maximum 57120 against the configured 57344), so a slightly lower max_model_len makes the boot deterministic; the native kernel-off against on pair is 26.5 against 25.7 ms with a paired acceptance interval of -8.9 to +2.1 percent (one boot per arm, eight prompts). The two expensive things on a 4090 under WSL2 stack: 23 at QMAX 8 with async, 56.5 at QMAX 16 synchronous (reproduction mode as the launcher ships it), 63.5 at QMAX 16 with async, so the launcher's coupling of synchronous scheduling to the long block is what keeps a reproduction-mode user off the worst combination. Unrelated to that kernel: the first-listed Triton configurations (VLLM_TRITON_FORCE_FIRST_CONFIG=1) run the sampled path at 45 to 47 ms on two boots on the 4090 and at 25 on the 3090, a matched-configuration asymmetry; the chunked GDN autotune race does not run at the default with a warm compile cache (the engine loads its artefacts; no autotune line in a default boot with TRITON_PRINT_AUTOTUNING=1, against about 110 for a new shape), which is also why issue 75's eight winner sets were seen only with VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE=1. One default-width boot in nine on the 4090 landed at 45 ms with clocks and link identical and its startup not captured; item 11 names the state behind it.

**10. Turning prefix caching off turns the mamba cache mode off too, with one warning line, and the engine's arguments line still reports the mode you passed.** vLLM's MambaModelConfig forces `mamba_cache_mode` to `none` whenever prefix caching is disabled (model_executor/models/config.py:632-633 in 0.28.0, one WARNING line), so `--mamba-cache-mode align` cannot be kept with the cache off, the launcher's coupling of the two flags (lines 442-447) is moot, and the API server's "non-default args" line shows `'mamba_cache_mode': 'align'` beside `'enable_prefix_caching': False` two seconds before the override, which misled two boxes for a night. We had attributed boot-to-boot reproducibility to the align mode from arms that were meant to keep it with the cache off; those arms ran in mode none, so that attribution is withdrawn and the mechanism of the divergence is open. What we can say: at the launcher default (cache on, mode align) boots replay on the recorded counters (drafts, accepted tokens, output length per row) across nine boots on the 4090 and two sampled pairs natively; with the cache off (mode none) boots sometimes replay and sometimes diverge, on both boxes, even when they load identical compiled artefacts. Two lines for the README would save the next reader the night: prefix caching off means mamba cache mode none on this model, and the warning is easy to miss because the earlier arguments line shows the passed value.

**11. With the fork's split-KV verify attention in the path, a boot that recompiles the DFlash2 head's graph while loading the backbone's from the compile cache runs the whole step 1.9x to 2.9x slow on two RTX 4090s in one WSL2 host, and the same boot is in band on a native 3090; whether the card, the PCIe link (x8 on one 4090 under load, unsampled on the other, x16 on the 3090) or the virtualization carries the cost is untested.** This is a predictive association on this host, not yet a mechanism: the rule called its fifth instance from the boot log before the step cost was read, and a boot in the same state with the verify kernel off was not slow. The engine compiles three graphs and caches each under its own key (the log names them: rank_0_0/backbone, rank_0_0/dflash_head, rank_0_0/dflash2_candidate_selector). When the head's key changes between two boots whose backbone key does not (the record shows this between boots whose non-default-args lines match), the next boot loads the backbone artefact and recompiles the head, and on the 4090s that boot runs at 45 ms per step against 23 on one card and 67 against 23 on the other, where a boot that loads all three or compiles all three runs at 23; the later boots that load the head it wrote run at 23. The diagnostic in the log is the head's compile time as a ratio: about two thirds of its full-compile time on the 4090s (8.6 to 8.9 s against 13.0 to 13.6) and about four sevenths on the 3090 (10.1 against 17.6) means the backbone was loaded; the absolute times are host-specific, the shortening is universal. This host: three such boots of three slow on card 0 (45.1, 44.9, 46.5), one forced into the state on purpose (45.1), and one on the second card by accident about thirty minutes later (67.1 against 22.9 for the boot before it); and at that configuration (width 7, verify kernel on, graphs on, autotuned kernels, cache enabled) twelve boots outside the class, seven full loads, three full compiles and two that loaded the head and recompiled only the selector, none slow. On the native 3090 the matched class runs inside its band: three accidental boots at 25.1 to 25.5 and one forced into the exact sequence (backbone loaded, head compiled, selector loaded) at 25.0, with the same shortened head compile (17.6 s after a compiled backbone, 10.1 s after a loaded one there; 13.0 and 8.9 s on the 4090), so the trigger is universal and visible in the log and the cost appears only on the 4090 host. One more boot narrows where the cost sits: a boot in the same state with the fork's split-KV verify attention off (SPEC_ATTN=0, width 15) was not slow, so the state costs only with that kernel in the path (one boot, registered for replication), and items 9 and 11 may be one effect with two triggers; a boot in the state at width 15 with the kernel on decides it (materially above 56.5 ms per step means two effects, near it means one), and on the 3090, where that kernel costs nothing at the settings tested within a 0.8 ms floor, the same boots read 25.1 and 26.7. This is the boot-state diagnostic under the "one default boot in nine landed at 45" observation in item 9 and, we think, under part of issue 75's per-boot scatter on this card: the state is set before the first request, prints no warning, and a user who changes any setting that re-keys only the head (we have not enumerated which) will boot into it once and read a 2x regression until the next boot. Two things a maintainer can do: print a line when a boot mixes loaded and compiled graphs, and check what the head's compiled graph assumes about the backbone's when the backbone was loaded rather than traced in the same process (the forced slow boot and the boot that then loaded the head it wrote have the same recorded trajectory on all 32 rows, drafts, accepted tokens and output length per row, at 45.1 and 23.3 ms per step, and one accidental slow boot matched the fast default on the same signature at 45, so speed and the recorded trajectory are separable; on the 3090 the forced sequence changed neither the step cost nor a token, 8 of 8 identical to normal boots).

**12. The compile cache key of at least one graph depends on whether the graph before it was loaded or compiled, and the cache grows down the chain; please inspect the key construction.** Upstream vLLM, not the fork. The engine caches its three compiled graphs separately. In a matched pair on the 4090 host, a boot that loaded the backbone's artefact and compiled the head's then loaded one selector artefact, and the next boot, which loaded that head, had to recompile the selector under a different key; the same happened in a pair on the native 3090. So the selector's key changed with nothing but the head's load-or-compile state, which is one reason a boot at an unchanged configuration can land in the partial state of item 11. A recursive size listing of the cache directories sorts the artefacts into three size classes, and on every volume the count rises down the chain: 15 backbone-class, 22 head-class and 25 selector-class artefacts on one 4090 volume (6.7 GB), 9, 16 and 21 on the other (3.7 GB), 16, 21 and 33 on the 3090 (6.8 GB). Whether every graph is keyed on the whole prior history, and so whether the directory grows with the number of distinct boot histories, is what the key construction would say; the counts are consistent with it and do not prove it.

**13. The launcher's memory defaults assume a quantized drafter of about 1.2 GiB, and a bf16 community drafter on a 24 GiB card cannot be booted without reading the launcher.** Three budgets are set independently and none knows the drafter's size: the KV pin (5.2 GiB fixed), GPU_UTIL (0.93), and the V2 runner's graph reservation (VLLM_V2_CUDAGRAPH_MEM_MIB, 1900). Two community drafters exist for this target (a plain DFlash block-16 head at 4.0 GiB bf16 and a DSpark head at 3.5 GiB), and on a 3090 five boots in a row failed at five different walls before one served: the pin plus the drafter does not fit (OOM at KV allocation); with the pin lowered the default 65536 context needs 5.18 GiB; MAX_LEN from the environment is honoured at line 150 and then overwritten on the DFlash path at line 360 by DFLASH_MAX_LEN, so the documented variable does nothing there; with the context lowered through the right variable, GPU_UTIL minus the target, the drafter and the graph reservation leaves under a gigabyte of KV; only a 4096 context served. None of the three error messages names the pin, the graph reservation or the second variable. Two lines in the README (the DFlash context variable, and that a bf16 drafter needs the pin and the reservation reconsidered) or a launcher check of the drafter's size against the budgets would save a user four boots. On a 4090 with 22.45 GiB free at the same point the pin fit, which is why this was found on the smaller card.

**14. The fork's split-KV verify attention breaks the plain DFlash path.** A plain DFlash checkpoint (DFlashDraftModel, method "dflash", the vLLM model class qwen3_dflash.py the image ships) loads and compiles through the launcher (DRAFT from the environment, LOOKUP=0), and with SPEC_ATTN=1 (the default) the engine dies at initialisation after graph capture with "RuntimeError: Triton Error [CUDA]: an illegal memory access was encountered"; with SPEC_ATTN=0 the same boot serves. [Pending: one 4090 boot each way; the kernel-on boot with a light memory profile decides whether the fault needs the kernel or the memory its buffers take; a 3090 kernel-on boot is queued.] Two further things about that path in this stack, drafter-independent: its graph capture reports "took 0.00 GiB" with FULL captures only and no PIECEWISE capture, where a DFlash2 boot captures both and takes 1.3 GiB, and the step then runs at about 146 ms against 23, launch-bound; so a plain DFlash drafter cannot be speed-compared to DFlash2 here, only acceptance-compared.

**Not for the note, recorded for us.** The boot-to-boot divergence on this box is confined, as observed, to reproduction mode (DFLASH_TOKENS=15): five width-15 boots of one configuration on card 0 fell into two observed groups, differing on the same ten rows in rounds and accepted tokens, and four cache-off boots of one configuration on card 1 fell into three, so it is not a binary state; nine boots at the launcher default are one trajectory on the recorded counters (drafts, accepted, output length; the cells keep no text); present on both cards of this WSL2 host at width 15, and on the native 3090 only with prefix caching off (eight cache-on boots one trajectory, three cache-off boots two groups; prefix caching off also sets the mamba cache mode to none, item 10); the Triton autotune race is not the sole source (force-first-config boots diverge more, not less, and the first-listed config runs twice as slow at width 7 on a 4090, and it produced empty completions on 2 of 32 rows at the launcher default, the same prompt and the same two seeds on a 4090 under WSL2 and on a native 3090, so VLLM_TRITON_FORCE_FIRST_CONFIG is neither a determinism fix nor a speed-up and can change the output, not only the timing; at greedy the same prompt runs to a full answer on both cards, so EOS is near the top of the first-token distribution under that config rather than on top of it: a caution about a flag people reach for to make behaviour more predictable: greedy excludes an EOS-argmax failure and the sampled-distribution question is unclassified, so not an upstream report), and card, driver, WSL2 and container are confounded between the boxes. The cross-box difference in acceptance level is inside one box's own sampling spread and is not a finding. At temperature 0 the two cards' completions part at a near-tie within the first thirty to sixty tokens of nearly every prompt, and forcing the Triton kernel configuration moves the greedy text by about as much as changing the card does, so the kernel configuration is a third axis beside card and boot, and identical text on two boxes was never a reasonable expectation even with everything else matched. Issue 75's scatter needs a different explanation than the one we gave it; do not reopen that thread until the cache pair, the trajectory count and the greedy pair land.
