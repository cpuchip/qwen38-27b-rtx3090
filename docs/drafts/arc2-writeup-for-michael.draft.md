## The question you asked

Is DFlash2's lead over MTP real, or a property of these cards, and is 3.3 of 7 leaving something on the table.

**Real, and it is the drafter (one boot per arm on the native 3090, this cohort).** At the width users run, DFlash2 accepts 31.5 percent more per step than MTP's default and gives 19.6 percent more throughput. At matched width 7 (MTP can be run at 7; the launcher's own default for it is 4), DFlash2 accepts 15.9 percent more per step and is 8.3 percent cheaper per step. The two drafters are indistinguishable at position 0 (78.9 against 79.0 percent) and the gap opens with depth: MTP has lost 40 percent of DFlash2's conditional rate by position 6. Widening MTP nets slightly worse throughput. For these two checkpoints the block-trained drafter stays useful at depth and the chained distilled heads do not.

**3.3 of 7 is what this drafter yields on this cohort, and the width is not what limits it.** Conditional on reaching a position, the drafter accepts at a roughly flat 70 to 81 percent at every one of its seven positions here (76 to 82 natively). The steep unconditional curve is that constant hazard multiplied out. Narrowing the verify block only loses accepted tokens (monotone 3 to 7) and does not lower step cost. A wider-trained drafter is the proposal this points at (the sweep did not test one), and one exists for a different target (a block-16 DFlash2 for Muse Glimmer), not for this one.

| split-KV verify attention at width 15, on against off | no detectable difference (25.7 against 26.5, one boot each, inside the spread) | 56.5 against 23.6 ms, 2.4x (five on, one off, the off boot with an early-EOS row) |

On bare metal graph capture is worth about 2x and the kernel choice makes no detectable difference; on the 4090 under WSL2 the kernel choice is worth 2x and the capture factor is not estimated (the two graphs-off boots split, and the slow side exists with every graph off, so lost capture is not its cause). The native box's three nulls each rest on one or two boots per arm with a 0.8 ms resolution floor; they exclude the 2x and 2.4x effects they sit beside and say nothing about a few percent. In one of the two graphs-off boots, the first-listed Triton configuration with full graph capture was slower on this card than autotuned kernels with no capture at all. Neither of those is a property of the fork; the third row is, and its switch waits on an off replicate.

## Determinism

The launcher default replayed to the token in every boot tested on both boxes: nine default boots here, all 32 rows identical between any two on the signature of drafts, accepted and output (drafted-token counts excluded, because the adaptive policy carries state across boots), and sampled pairs on the native 3090. That is an observation on this cohort, seed set, cache state and configuration, not a proof. On the 3090 at width 7, two boots with the cache off and the mamba align mode kept were identical on all eight prompts, and two with the align mode off differed on all eight: in that one experiment align, not prefix caching, went with the replay, and the launcher sets align only as a side effect of PREFIX_CACHE=1 (item 10 of the note, which now asks Mads to confirm with counterbalanced boots before moving the flag). On this box's card 1 at width 15, a cache-off pair agreed on 17 of 32 rows against 29 of 32 for a cache-on pair, two separate pairs rather than a randomized assignment; whether that is the card or the width is one queued cell. Reproduction mode does not replay here: five width-15 boots on card 0 fell into two observed groups differing on the same ten rows (two groups at n=5, not a proof of a binary state); card 1's three forced boots at three thresholds gave three vectors (three configurations, one boot each); natively, width 15 gave one trajectory across eight boots with prefix caching on and two groups across three with it off, and that cache-off arm removed align with the cache (confirmed from the cell source), so it does not show prefix caching hiding anything; the one-variable native pair at width 15 (cache off, align kept) is running as this is written. Skipping the Triton autotune race made boots diverge more (6 of 32 rows identical) and turning the cache off did the same (17 of 32), so neither is the sole source; the source is not named. It never flipped a greedy argmax. Issue 75 narrows to the width-15 path.

## What the night cost and taught

Instruments were corrected four times: the launcher inside the image is the fork's (LOOKUP is the alias it honours); the engine's own non-default-args line is the resolved configuration; a rule that two engines on this host slow each other was written from one boot pair and withdrawn when the repeat landed on the other side with the neighbour idle; and the step cost on this card has two separate stories: the width-15 cost is one named kernel (the split-KV verify attention: off means 23.6 ms in one boot that also produced an early-EOS row), and the forced first Triton configuration runs the sampled path slow on this card and not on the 3090 (a matched-configuration asymmetry; the GDN race itself never runs at the default with a warm compile cache). One default-width boot in nine landed slow (45 against 23, clocks and graphs identical), a single observation that is recorded and not claimed. A script of mine truncated the validation record to four lines for about six hours (it opened the file for writing before reading it); the second review caught it and the file was restored from its parent commit with nothing lost. Issue 75 gets the first half with its price. Journal lessons 49 to 82.
