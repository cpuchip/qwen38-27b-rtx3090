Everything below is in docs/v0.28-validation.md from the heading "Arc two: throughput", with three fresh-eyes reviews beside it (docs/reviews/arc2-fresh-eyes-opus.md, arc2-fresh-eyes-sol.md, arc2-fresh-eyes-sol-3.md). Corrections are appended in the record's own order; the five historical readings the second review named carry a bracketed pointer in place, and every later withdrawal is an appended correction. Numbers carry their conditions. Three conventions for reading: "card 0" and "card 1" are the two RTX 4090s in this WSL2 host; cell names such as std7 or r7e are the raw file stems in projects/flightbench/results/raw/v028, where every local number can be recomputed from the ROW lines; every native 3090 number is reported from threadchip's private logs and cannot be verified from this tree.

## The question you asked

Is DFlash2's lead over MTP real, or a property of these cards, and is 3.3 of 7 (3.3 accepted tokens per verification step out of seven drafted positions) leaving something on the table.

**Real, and it is the drafter.** One boot per arm on the native 3090, this eight-prompt cohort, reported from private logs. At the width users run, DFlash2 accepts 31.5 percent more per step than MTP's default and gives 19.6 percent more throughput. At matched width 7 (MTP can be run at 7; the launcher's own default for it is 4), DFlash2 accepts 15.9 percent more per step and is 8.3 percent cheaper per step. The two drafters are indistinguishable at position 0 (78.9 against 79.0 percent) and the gap opens with depth: MTP has lost 40 percent of DFlash2's conditional rate by position 6. Widening MTP nets slightly worse throughput. For these two checkpoints the block-trained drafter stays useful at depth and the chained distilled heads do not.

**3.3 of 7 is what this drafter yields on this cohort, and the width is not what limits it.** Conditional on reaching a position, the drafter accepts at a roughly flat 70 to 81 percent at every one of its seven positions on card 0 (five boots at widths 3 to 7, one boot each) and 76 to 82 percent on the native 3090 (one boot, reported). The steep unconditional curve is that constant hazard multiplied out. Narrowing the verify block only loses accepted tokens (monotone across widths 3 to 7, one boot each) and does not lower the step cost within those boots' 3.7 percent spread. A wider-trained drafter is the proposal this points at; the sweep did not test one, and the block-16 DFlash2 that exists for a different target (Muse Glimmer) is an example that such drafters are trained, not evidence for this one.

**Reproduction mode's step cost, in full.** The cost is named: the fork's split-KV verify attention compiled with a query maximum of 16. Width 7 with that maximum forced runs at 63.5 ms per step against 23.2, both with async scheduling on and only the maximum differing (a maximum of 9 and of 12 run at 23.2 and 23.4, so the step is between 13 and 16); the 3090 reads 25.0 against 25.1 on the same pair (reported). This establishes a cost that depends on the maximum, not why the specialization is slow. With the kernel off (SPEC_ATTN=0) width 15 runs at 23.6 ms per step on card 0 on two boots, both, with a similar aggregate yield among the active rows (3.732 accepted tokens per step against 3.72 to 3.74 with the kernel on; the per-position curve differs by a few percent at the first positions). Both boots return a one-token completion on prompt 5 seed 2, where every kernel-on boot returns 1024 and the greedy answer is full; the same switch at width 7 leaves that seed intact (one boot on card 0, four seeds each way on the 3090, reported), and the forced Triton configuration at width 7 empties it too. So it is a boundary seed that two of the tested numerics changes moved and one did not, not a defect of the switch, and at width 15 the workaround is not output-neutral on this cohort. Two practical notes for anyone taking it: the native kernel-off against on pair (26.5 against 25.7 ms, one boot per arm, eight prompts, reported) cannot exclude an 8.9 percent acceptance loss; and width 15 at the launcher's defaults sits about 10 MiB from the KV-cache edge on a 24 GiB card (two local boots booted, one native attempt at the same settings failed with a hard error, reported), so a slightly lower max_model_len makes the boot deterministic. Separately, the first-listed Triton configurations (VLLM_TRITON_FORCE_FIRST_CONFIG=1) run the sampled path at 45 to 47 ms per step on card 0 (two boots) and at 25 on the 3090 (two boots, reported), a matched-configuration asymmetry. One default-width boot in nine on card 0 had also landed at 45 instead of 23; that is the boot state named two sections below.

On bare metal graph capture is worth about 2x and the kernel choice makes no detectable difference; on card 0 the kernel choice is worth 2x and the capture factor is not estimated (the two graphs-off boots split, and the slow side exists with every graph off, so lost capture is not its cause). The three native nulls each rest on one or two boots per arm with a 0.8 ms resolution floor: they exclude the 2x and 2.4x effects they sit beside and say nothing about a few percent, and they are not independent of each other, because if the verify kernel costs nothing there within that floor at the settings tested, a differently built copy of it cannot cost much there either. In one of the two graphs-off boots, the first-listed Triton configuration with full graph capture was slower on card 0 than autotuned kernels with no capture at all. Neither of those is a property of the fork; the third row is, and its switch is a workaround with the output caveat above.

## The slow boot, named

One default-width boot in nine on card 0 had landed at 45 ms per step instead of 23 with everything the counters could see identical, and the graphs-off pair had done the same (33.7 then 46.5). Threadchip recalled a finding of mine from 2026-09-02 (a shared compile cache whose artefact made every boot that loaded it slow) and proposed that the slow boots are the ones that load part of their compiled graphs from the cache and recompile the rest. The engine compiles three graphs (the log names them backbone, dflash_head and dflash2_candidate_selector) and caches each under its own key. Joining the cache volume's inventory (every artefact with its write time, plus the two moved aside for a cell, with their original times) to each cell's boot window classified every local boot whose cell file carries an engine timestamp, without a new run (analyze_lineage.py in the raw directory; the card is read from the engine's port; the sequence is read from the startup capture where one exists, else from the artefacts written during the boot). At width 7 with the verify kernel on, graphs on, autotuned kernels and the cache enabled: twelve boots outside the class (seven full loads, three full compiles, two that loaded the head and recompiled only the selector), none slow; three boots that loaded the backbone's artefact and compiled the head's (std7 and a7off2 by accident, r7e on purpose), all slow at 44.9 to 45.1. The class members at other settings: the forced first configuration 45.0 (slow anyway), graphs off 46.5 against 33.7 for a graphs-off full compile, width 15 with async on 84.9, card 1 at width 7 with the cache off 67.1 against 22.9 for the full compile before it, and width 15 with the verify kernel off 23.6, not slow.

The boot forced into the state on purpose (r7e, the head's and selector's artefacts moved aside) ran at 45.1 against the registered 45, with one thing to say against the registration: its validity condition was written as two graphs compiled and one loaded, and the boot loaded an older selector artefact and compiled only the head, so the prediction landed on a boot the registration's letter would have called void; it is the cleaner single-graph case, and it is reported as that. The card-1 boot (p7b) fell into the state by accident about thirty minutes later, with card 0 idle, and was called from its boot log before its rows were timed. Both local cards are RTX 4090s, so the penalty is 1.9x on card 0 and 2.9x on card 1, a range between identical cards, not a fixed factor; card 0 runs at x8 Gen4 under load (sampled every 30 s), card 1's link under load was not sampled (its health-time reading came from card 0's instrument, and it reads x4 at idle), and the 3090 sits at x16, so whether the card, the link or the WSL2 layer carries the cost is untested. On the native 3090 threadchip found the same class three times by accident and once on purpose with the exact sequence, all inside its band at 25.0 to 25.5 (reported).

One thing is universal: the head compiles about 40 percent faster after a loaded backbone on both boxes (17.6 to 10.1 s there, reported; 13.0 to 13.6 against 8.6 to 8.9 s here, five boots), so the trigger is deterministic and visible in the log before the first token, and the head's compile time at about two thirds of its full-compile time is the tell; the cost appears only on this host. The last cell of the night narrowed where it sits: a boot that fell into the same state with the fork's split-KV verify attention off was not slow (one boot at width 15), so in the sampled boots the state costs only with that kernel in the path, which ties the two step-cost findings of the arc to one kernel. Whether they are one effect with two triggers is a hypothesis for the registered cell below, not a conclusion. The artefact such a boot writes is fine (its inductor kernel files and best-config match a full compile's, compared by hand and not in the inventory, and the boots that later load it are fast); the process that compiled it is what runs slow. Speed and the recorded trajectory are separable: the forced slow boot and the boot that loaded the head it wrote have the same recorded trajectory on all 32 rows (drafts, accepted tokens and output length per row; the files do not keep text) at 45.1 and 23.3 ms per step, std7 matches the fast default on the same signature at 45, and on the 3090 the forced sequence changed neither the step cost nor, by threadchip's text comparison, a token (reported). Why the head's key changes between boots whose engine configuration lines match, what the shorter compile skips, and why the result is slow only on this host are the maintainer's questions; item 11 of the note carries them with the numbers. This is the observed boot-state diagnostic under the "one boot in nine" observation above, a strong predictive association on this host rather than an internal mechanism, and we think it sits under part of issue 75's scatter on this card.

Why boots at an unchanged configuration land partial at all is a second observation, upstream of the fork and checkable with a directory listing: in the r7e and r7f pair the selector asked for a different artefact when the head was compiled than when it was loaded, and threadchip saw the same in his pair (reported), so the cache key of at least one graph depends on the load-or-compile state of the graph before it. The recursive size listing beside the inventory (aot-sizeclass-20260905.txt) sorts the artefacts into three size classes, and on every volume the count rises down the chain: 15 backbone-class, 22 head-class and 25 selector-class artefacts on lane 0 (6.7 GB), 9, 16 and 21 on lane 1 (3.7 GB), and 16, 21 and 33 on the 3090 (6.8 GB, reported). Whether every graph is keyed on the whole prior history, and so whether the cache grows with the number of distinct boot histories, is for the key construction to say; item 12 of the note asks for that inspection. ## Determinism

The launcher default replayed on the recorded counters in every boot tested on both boxes: nine default boots on card 0, all 32 rows identical between any two on the signature of drafts, accepted tokens and output length (drafted-token counts excluded, because the adaptive policy carries state across boots; the files do not keep text), and two sampled pairs on the native 3090 (reported). That is an observation on this cohort, seed set, cache state and configuration, not a proof. Everything we had said about the mamba align mode fell in the last hour of the night: vLLM forces the mamba cache mode to none whenever prefix caching is off, with one warning line that is easy to miss because the engine's earlier arguments line still shows the mode you passed, so every "cache off, align kept" arm on both boxes ran in mode none, and the native result that align was the determinism flag has no arm behind it (threadchip read his logs, found the warning in exactly those arms, and retracted it). Item 10 of the note now says only what stands. What the same boots do show, at low power: with prefix caching on, every boot tested on either box replayed; with it off, two of four native pairs were identical and two split (reported), and on card 1 one identical pair appears among the six pairwise comparisons of four boots, so cache-off boots are sometimes reproducible and sometimes not, and no mechanism is named. Reproduction mode does not replay on card 0: five width-15 boots with the cache on fell into two observed groups differing on the same ten rows, and four cache-off boots at one exact configuration on card 1 fell into three groups while two of them loaded identical compiled artefacts, so the state is not binary and not the compile lineage alone; natively, width 15 gave one trajectory across eight boots with prefix caching on and split with it off (reported). Skipping the Triton autotune race made boots diverge more (6 of 32 rows identical) and turning the cache off did the same (17 of 32), so neither is the sole source; the source is not named. No boot state flipped a greedy argmax in the greedy cells (g7r and g7s replay each other on 32 of 32 rows, and the forced configuration at greedy runs prompt 5 to a full answer on both boxes). Issue 75 narrows to the width-15 path and, for the step cost, to the boot state named above.

## What the night cost and taught

Instruments were corrected five times. The launcher inside the image is the fork's (LOOKUP is the alias it honours). The engine's non-default-args line is an echo of what was passed, not the resolved configuration, and any setting the engine can derive or replace needs its own downstream confirmation, which is how a night's align story rested on arms that never existed (lesson 90). A rule that two engines on this host slow each other was written from one boot pair and withdrawn when the repeat landed on the other side with the neighbour idle. The step cost on card 0 has two stories that may be one: the width-15 cost is one named kernel (the split-KV verify attention: off means 23.6 ms on two boots, both with the early-EOS row), and the boot state above is slow only with that kernel in the path. And the forced first Triton configuration runs the sampled path slow on card 0 and not on the 3090, a matched-configuration asymmetry; the GDN autotune race itself never runs at the default with a warm compile cache, and when it does run it does not converge on card 0 (four cold races, four different winner sets, at most one kernel of six on its first-listed configuration). The one default-width boot in nine that landed slow (45 against 23, clocks identical, its startup not captured) is the boot state above. A script of mine truncated the validation record to six lines for about six hours (it opened the file for writing before reading it); the second review caught it and the file was restored from its parent commit with nothing lost.
