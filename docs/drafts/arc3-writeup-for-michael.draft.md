# Arc three, the community drafters (draft for Michael, 2026-09-05, cells still running are marked)

Everything below is in docs/v0.28-validation.md from the heading "Arc three: drafters", with the same conventions as arc two: card 0 and card 1 are the two RTX 4090s in this WSL2 host; cell names are the raw file stems in projects/flightbench/results/raw/v028 and every local number can be recomputed from their ROW lines with analyze_drafters.py; every native 3090 number is reported from threadchip's private logs. The three free things you named: the block sweep below 7 was already measured in arc two (widths 3 to 7 lose accepted tokens monotonically at the same step cost, so 7 stays); the two community drafters are this arc; the hidden-state fine-tune comes after it.

## The answer

**Width 15 is not where the missing throughput is.** On the 3090, matched at an 8192 context, DFlash2 at width 15 with the lookup on gives 151.6 tok/s against 149.5 at width 7, 1.4 percent, one boot per arm; on card 0 the same mode read 56.5 against 23.2 ms per step in arc two and was called a kernel cost; re-measured under the adapter counters, that boot had about 420 MB of its working set over this host's dedicated ceiling, and the same width with the same kernel at a pin that fits runs at 23.4 ms per step with the same acceptance, 157.8 against 160.4 tok/s for width 7 at that pin. So width 15 costs neither card anything per step once its working set is on the card, and buys neither card more than a percent or two. The lookup tail earns 0.6 percent of accepted tokens at 32 rows (positions 7 to 14 of a width-15 block), which cannot buy more than a percent or two of throughput, and does not. Arc two said why (the seam sits at the head's block size minus one; the head covers seven positions); this prices the whole mode end to end.

**The block-16 community drafter loses to the DFlash2 head, and the reason is depth, not width.** At width 7, the width users run, it is worse on both axes: on the 3090, matched configuration and normal graph capture, 3.000 against 3.832 accepted tokens per step as run (paired -0.832, t -3.30, worse on 25 of 32 rows; -0.522 with the two one-token boundary rows excluded, the figure that compares across boxes) and 30.9 against 25.6 ms per step, about 65 percent of DFlash2's throughput; card 0 agrees on acceptance: -0.64 tokens per step against the shipped configuration and -0.57 against the lookup-off baseline on a full 32-row cell with the kernel on at a fitting pin (t -4.2 and -3.5; -0.55 and -0.46 with the boundary row excluded, so both boxes sit at about -0.5 on the comparable figure), and the kernel itself does not move acceptance (kernel on against off on the same rows, -0.02); card 1 then agreed with card 0 to a hundredth of a token per step on the same drafter (a clean 32-row cell at the fitting pin, +0.01 against card 0's), so the acceptance numbers are a property of the drafter, not of a card or a kernel setting. At its own trained width, 15 (card 0, 11 rows, the fork's verify kernel off): its trained tail contributes 8.3 percent of its accepted tokens where DFlash2's lookup tail contributes 0.6 percent, about 14 times as a share (3.2 times per round), so a wide trained block is worth something; and it still loses the total, 3.17 against 3.80 tokens per step, because positions 0 to 6 are where the tokens are and it is worse at every one of them. Its acceptance falls faster with depth than DFlash2's (232 to 26 per thousand rounds by position 5 against 201 to 54) and giving it its full block did not flatten that curve, so the "it only lost at 7 because it was built for 15" reading did not survive: the deficit has the shape MTP had in arc two. Caveats that ride with it: one boot per cell, 11 to 32 rows, and all three drafters run against a W4A16 target that none of them was trained against (the DFlash2 head on bf16 by its provenance, the block-16 drafter on an NVFP4 target, DSpark evaluated on NVFP4 and FP8), so the target mismatch is shared and is not our drafter's advantage.

**DSpark, the only comparison symmetric on width (its block and DFlash2's both give seven drafts), and our drafter wins it.** On the 3090, both arms at the reduced profile the community drafters need (an 8192 context, 0.97 utilization, no pin), 32 rows each, one boot per arm: DSpark 3.374 accepted tokens per step and 29.5 ms per step against DFlash2's 3.832 and 25.6, 114 against 150 tok/s, 76 percent of DFlash2's throughput (paired -0.458, t -2.98, worse on 26 of 32 rows), with no width excuse available to either side and the target mismatch shared. The finding underneath is better than either headline: DSpark, the block-16 drafter and MTP all show one signature against the DFlash2 head, ahead or level at the first position and falling away with depth (DSpark's per-position ratio to DFlash2 runs 1.05, 1.01, 0.97, 0.93, 0.85, 0.82, 0.80 from position 0 to 6; the block-16 drafter's curve does the same here; MTP fell 1.00 to 0.60 in arc two), and DFlash2 is the only drafter of the four that holds its acceptance with depth. DSpark, trained for exactly 7, decays that way too, so the depth behaviour is not a width artefact in either community drafter. Why the block-trained head holds where the others thin is a hypothesis, not a measurement. Card 0 then ran the same comparison at the fitting profile (a 3 GiB pin, 8192 context), with both boots full compiles and the adapter counters showing DSpark's working set on the card with about 440 MB to spare, the first community-drafter boot on this card with room: DSpark 3.191 accepted tokens per step and 27.5 ms per step against the DFlash2 head's 3.773 and 23.3 at the same profile, 115.1 against 160.4 tok/s (paired -0.582, t -4.91, worse on 28 of 32 rows), per-position curve 197 134 94 68 52 39 29 against the head's level one. So the symmetric comparison is two-card, and with the boot class and the memory state matched the two hosts agree on the drafter to a few percent: acceptance -0.58 here and -0.46 there, step cost 1.18 times here and 1.15 there, 115 tok/s here and 114 there. That agreement is the strongest evidence this arc has that the earlier cross-host disagreements were boot class and memory, not hosts.

## What it cost to find out, which is a finding on its own

- **A bf16 community drafter does not fit a 24 GiB card at the launcher's defaults, and the errors do not say why.** Three memory budgets are set independently (the fixed 5.2 GiB KV pin, gpu_memory_utilization at 0.93, the 1.9 GiB graph reservation) and none knows the drafter's size; a 1.2 GiB W4A16 head fits in the slack and a 4.0 GiB bf16 one does not. On the 3090 seven boots hit seven different walls (the pin; the pin lowered; MAX_LEN honoured at line 150 and overwritten on the DFlash path at line 360 by DFLASH_MAX_LEN; a 16384 context; an 8192 context at width 7; 8192 and 4096 at width 15), and the walls explain themselves: halving the context moved the requirement by 0.36 GiB, so about 1.5 GiB is fixed per-request state, the mamba page of this hybrid model, which the context barely touches while every error message recommends shrinking it. The number of sequence slots is not a lever either: one slot made the available memory smaller (1.41 to 1.23 GiB) while the requirement stayed at 1.87, so the state is charged per request at admission, and the list is exhausted short of a utilization nobody recommends. The result on that card, same context and utilization at width 15: DFlash2's 1.2 GiB head leaves 4.12 GiB for the KV cache and serves; the 4.0 GiB bf16 drafter leaves 1.41 and cannot serve at any context tried. A drafter published for exactly this target cannot run at its trained width on the 24 GiB card class most users have. Card 0 reached it only through a 5.85 GB pin at a 57344 context that the 3090 cannot reproduce, because card 0 boots it on the pinned path over-committed. So the two cards split the answer and neither could give both: card 0 has the acceptance at width 15, the 3090 has the deployability. The ladder on the 3090 at one profile (8192, 0.97 utilization, no pin) is the cleanest form of the item: beside 1.2, 3.5 and 4.0 GiB of drafter the engine reports 4.51, 2.36 and 1.74 GiB of KV cache available, serving, serving, and serving at width 7 but not at 15; and the pool is only half of it. At the shipped defaults (the 5.20 GiB pin, a 65536 context, width 7) on the 3090, from the engine's own lines: beside the 1.2 GiB head one 65536-token request needs 4.97 GiB of KV cache and serves with about five percent of the pin to spare; beside the 3.5 GiB DSpark head the same request needs 7.05 GiB and the boot fails at that allocation; the requirement moved by 2.08 GiB when only the drafter changed. So a bigger drafter is charged twice, once as resident weights and again as a larger per-request KV requirement, and the shipped pin is sized for the small one with a five percent margin, which is also why a width-15 boot of the shipped head failed by ten mebibytes earlier in the day. That the requirement grew by about the size of the weights difference is two points, not a law; the mechanism is the measurement. Both community drafters fail at the defaults on both cards. On card 0 the renamed DSpark copy loaded as the Qwen3 class (17.58 GiB) and died at the same allocation, so the 3090's DSpark row above is a reduced-profile result, not a card that cleared a bar this one did not.
- **On this host an over-committed KV pin does not fail, it runs three to five times slower.** The boots of the block-16 drafter that served at 146 to 164 ms per step were the ones whose pinned KV cache, beside the 4 GiB drafter and the graph reservation, exceeded the card (measured on both cards by the adapter counters: shared usage 1.8 to 1.9 GB and total committed 25.2 to 25.3 GB on a 24.6 GB card, against a shared baseline of 86 MB; the width-11 and width-15 acceptance cells ran in that state, which does not touch acceptance); on the native 3090 the same configuration is a hard OOM at allocation, on WSL2 it runs, because GPU allocations beyond the card spill into host memory. The pair was then measured on one card with the counters watching both halves: the same drafter, same card, same kernel setting, over-committed at 264 ms per step with 1.8 GB spilled, and at a fitting pin 40 ms per step with 86 MB, a factor of 6.5 from the pin and the context alone. At a pin that fits, the same drafter with the fork's verify kernel on serves at 49.4 ms per step against the DFlash2 head's 23.3 at the same pin and context, where the 3090's pair reads 30.9 against 25.6; but the two pairs are not comparable, because the 3090's boots were full compiles and the 4090's drafter boot was a mixed one (backbone artefact loaded, head compiled in-process, the arc-two slow class, which costs the 4090 about twice the step and the 3090 nothing; the same scan found the 3090's width-15 boot was a mixed boot too, at a normal 26.2 ms per step), and the counters had that boot's rows at the dedicated ceiling with 130 MiB in shared memory. The plain-DFlash boot is being repeated with its head artefact in the cache; near 30 ms per step means the residual was the boot class and the two boxes agree on the path's cost, near 49 means a real path cost with both confounders removed. Until it lands the cross-card residual is withdrawn. So a WSL2 user gets a silently slow server where a native user gets an error, and the memory item and this one are the same shortfall reported two ways. The arc-two family of host properties keeps its two members (the width-15 kernel cost, the slow-boot state) and this is not a third.
- **A published Qwen3 DSpark drafter cannot be served by vLLM 0.28 as published.** Two independent upstream defects hit before it loads, on both boxes at the same lines: the loader asks for the draft's quantization through the target's composed overrides and refuses them, and the model registry routes the checkpoint's architecture name to the DeepSeek V4 class. One line and one renamed config get past them; neither error names the cause.
- **The one illegal memory access** (the block-16 drafter with the fork's verify kernel on, card 0, the over-committed pin) did not reproduce once the pin fit: the same boot with the kernel on served. That was the memory state, not the kernel.
- **One request of the cohort is a boundary.** Prompt 5 seed 2 returned one token under the forced Triton configuration in arc two on both boxes, one token under the block-16 drafter on the 3090, and was the request on which two of three spilled boots of that drafter died with a CUDA illegal memory access here (the third died two requests earlier, also in the second seed's pass, also spilled); the clean boots of both drafters run it to completion. The reading with support is that the request sits at a decision boundary that any numerics change tips, and that a spilled host turns the tip into a fault where a clean one produces a short completion. The check ran: the same drafter at width 11 at the 3 GiB pin with the verify kernel on served all 32 rows with no fault and ran that request to a full completion, with its counters about 190 MB over the ceiling; so being over the line is not by itself sufficient. The three faulting boots were both far over it (1.8 to 1.9 GB in host memory) and kernel-off; the kernel-off twin then ran the whole cohort too, with its working set on the card (the verify kernel's capture costs about a gigabyte, and without it this drafter fits at the 3 GiB pin), and completed that request at 4.4 accepted tokens per step. So the fault belongs to the deeply spilled state, not to the kernel setting or the width: clean, the boundary request completes; spilled by a gigabyte or more, it faults. The numbers that matter are quoted with and without the boundary rows, and the step-cost means exclude them (a two-token completion is one step whose wall is the first-step latency).
- **Lookup drafting at width 7** is worth between about minus 4 and plus 2 percent of accepted tokens on this cohort at unchanged step cost, the two boxes disagreeing on the sign inside that band (one boot per arm each). Not a finding; a sentence beside item 6 of the note.

## Three tiers of numbers on this box, stated

The spill moves a working set into host memory; it does not change the computation, so it affects step costs and not acceptance. Every acceptance column in this arc stands as read, including the trained-tail result the answer rests on, and only the step costs of spilled cells are void. Card 0's numbers now come in three tiers: measured clean under the adapter counters (the DFlash2 head and the block-16 drafter at the fitting pin, and now the default configuration itself, re-measured at 23.2 ms per step with the counters at baseline); measured spilled (the over-committed boots, whose step costs are void and whose acceptance is intact); and measured before the counters existed, which is the largest tier and includes all of arc two. Those are not wrong, they are unchecked, and the check is now cheap: the three arc-two numbers that carry the most weight (the width-15 kernel cost, the default, the forced slow boot) are re-measured under the counters with an idle gate before each boot, and the rest are marked unverified rather than implied clean. All three have landed. The default reproduces at 23.2 ms per step with the counters at baseline, so it stands clean. The forced slow-class boot reproduces at 45.0 ms per step with 358 MB in shared memory and dedicated at its ceiling, 318 MB more committed than the same configuration with its graphs loaded: the slow-boot state has a memory face, about 260 MB over the ceiling. The width-15 cost reproduces at 56.5 ms per step with 512 MB in shared memory against an 86 MB baseline, steady from the first row: item 9's 4090 number was measured with about half a gigabyte of its working set off-card. The same width with the same kernel at a fitting pin then ran at 23.4 ms per step with identical acceptance (paired +0.01 tokens per step) and 2 GB of room: item 9's 4090 number was the residency, and the correction to the posted note is drafted for your pen (below). The width-7 cell that item 9 used to pin the cost on the kernel's query maximum then re-ran at the fitting profile at 23.2 ms per step with 2 GB of room, so that clause is certain too; and because a query maximum of 16 changes the head's compile key, that boot was also a mixed-class boot (backbone loaded, head compiled in-process), the state arc two posted as item 11, running at the fast number with room where the same class at the default pin ran at 45.0 with 260 MB over the line. Arc two's two host-only findings were one effect with two triggers, and the effect is host memory. Both of arc two's 4090-only slow states coincide with the working set crossing the ceiling and neither clean number does; coincidence is not cause, and the cells that give each state room decide it. The re-checks also give the mechanism under every memory finding on this host: the adapter's dedicated ceiling is about 23.37 GB of the card's 24.56, the default configuration sits within about 470 MB of it, and any larger working set (a wider block, a bigger drafter, a raised pin) goes into host memory instead of failing. The 3090 has no third tier: it cannot spill, so every native number in both arcs was taken in a state the counters would call clean, which is why its results serve as controls; not because the card is faster or slower, but because its failure mode is loud.

## On training a drafter, from the research

The record's answer stands: the DFlash2 head is five full-width layers (about 1.2 GB at 4-bit), DFlash's recipe is public and DFlash2's is not, SpecForge trains both with a two-GPU reference topology, two 4090s can do a warm-started fine-tune against the W4A16 target (3 to 15 dollars a run rented) and cannot train from scratch, and the community block-16 drafter that this arc measured was the free version of the "wider block" question. What this arc adds: a wider block is worth about one percent of throughput at best on this cohort even where it is free, so the fine-tune worth paying for is the one aimed at positions 0 to 6 (the quantized-target hidden-state hypothesis), not at width.
