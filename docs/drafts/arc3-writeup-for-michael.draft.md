# Arc three, the community drafters (draft for Michael, 2026-09-05, cells still running are marked)

Everything below is in docs/v0.28-validation.md from the heading "Arc three: drafters", with the same conventions as arc two: card 0 and card 1 are the two RTX 4090s in this WSL2 host; cell names are the raw file stems in projects/flightbench/results/raw/v028 and every local number can be recomputed from their ROW lines with analyze_drafters.py; every native 3090 number is reported from threadchip's private logs. The three free things you named: the block sweep below 7 was already measured in arc two (widths 3 to 7 lose accepted tokens monotonically at the same step cost, so 7 stays); the two community drafters are this arc; the hidden-state fine-tune comes after it.

## The answer

**Width 15 is not where the missing throughput is.** On the 3090, matched at an 8192 context, DFlash2 at width 15 with the lookup on gives 151.6 tok/s against 149.5 at width 7, 1.4 percent, one boot per arm; on card 0 the same mode pays the query-maximum-16 kernel cost (56.5 against 23.2 ms per step) and is a loss. The lookup tail earns 0.6 percent of accepted tokens at 32 rows (positions 7 to 14 of a width-15 block), which cannot buy more than a percent or two of throughput, and does not. Arc two said why (the seam sits at the head's block size minus one; the head covers seven positions); this prices the whole mode end to end.

**The block-16 community drafter loses to the DFlash2 head, and the reason is depth, not width.** At width 7, the width users run, it is worse on both axes: on the 3090, matched configuration and normal graph capture, 3.000 against 3.832 accepted tokens per step (paired -0.832, t -3.30, worse on 25 of 32 rows) and 30.9 against 25.6 ms per step, about 65 percent of DFlash2's throughput; card 0 agrees on acceptance: -0.64 tokens per step against the shipped configuration and -0.57 against the lookup-off baseline on a full 32-row cell with the kernel on at a fitting pin (t -4.2 and -3.5), and the kernel itself does not move acceptance (kernel on against off on the same rows, -0.02). At its own trained width, 15 (card 0, 11 rows, the fork's verify kernel off): its trained tail contributes 8.3 percent of its accepted tokens where DFlash2's lookup tail contributes 0.6 percent, about 14 times as a share (3.2 times per round), so a wide trained block is worth something; and it still loses the total, 3.17 against 3.80 tokens per step, because positions 0 to 6 are where the tokens are and it is worse at every one of them. Its acceptance falls faster with depth than DFlash2's (232 to 26 per thousand rounds by position 5 against 201 to 54) and giving it its full block did not flatten that curve, so the "it only lost at 7 because it was built for 15" reading did not survive: the deficit has the shape MTP had in arc two. Caveats that ride with it: one boot per cell, 11 to 32 rows, and all three drafters run against a W4A16 target that none of them was trained against (the DFlash2 head on bf16 by its provenance, the block-16 drafter on an NVFP4 target, DSpark evaluated on NVFP4 and FP8), so the target mismatch is shared and is not our drafter's advantage.

**DSpark, the only comparison symmetric on width (its block and DFlash2's both give seven drafts), and our drafter wins it.** On the 3090, matched configuration, 32 rows each, one boot per arm: DSpark 3.374 accepted tokens per step and 29.5 ms per step against DFlash2's 3.832 and 25.6, 114 against 150 tok/s, 76 percent of DFlash2's throughput (paired -0.458, t -2.98, worse on 26 of 32 rows), with no width excuse available to either side and the target mismatch shared. The finding underneath is better than either headline: DSpark, the block-16 drafter and MTP all show one signature against the DFlash2 head, ahead or level at the first position and falling away with depth (DSpark's per-position ratio to DFlash2 runs 1.05, 1.01, 0.97, 0.93, 0.85, 0.82, 0.80 from position 0 to 6; the block-16 drafter's curve does the same here; MTP fell 1.00 to 0.60 in arc two), and DFlash2 is the only drafter of the four that holds its acceptance with depth. DSpark, trained for exactly 7, decays that way too, so the depth behaviour is not a width artefact in either community drafter. Why the block-trained head holds where the others thin is a hypothesis, not a measurement. [Card 0's DSpark boots on the renamed copy are pending.]

## What it cost to find out, which is a finding on its own

- **A bf16 community drafter does not fit a 24 GiB card at the launcher's defaults, and the errors do not say why.** Three memory budgets are set independently (the fixed 5.2 GiB KV pin, gpu_memory_utilization at 0.93, the 1.9 GiB graph reservation) and none knows the drafter's size; a 1.2 GiB W4A16 head fits in the slack and a 4.0 GiB bf16 one does not. On the 3090 seven boots hit seven different walls (the pin; the pin lowered; MAX_LEN honoured at line 150 and overwritten on the DFlash path at line 360 by DFLASH_MAX_LEN; a 16384 context; an 8192 context at width 7; 8192 and 4096 at width 15), and the walls explain themselves: halving the context moved the requirement by 0.36 GiB, so about 1.5 GiB is fixed per-request state, the mamba page of this hybrid model, which the context barely touches while every error message recommends shrinking it. The number of sequence slots is not a lever either: one slot made the available memory smaller (1.41 to 1.23 GiB) while the requirement stayed at 1.87, so the state is charged per request at admission, and the list is exhausted short of a utilization nobody recommends. The result on that card, same context and utilization at width 15: DFlash2's 1.2 GiB head leaves 4.12 GiB for the KV cache and serves; the 4.0 GiB bf16 drafter leaves 1.41 and cannot serve at any context tried. A drafter published for exactly this target cannot run at its trained width on the 24 GiB card class most users have. Card 0 reached it only through a 5.85 GB pin at a 57344 context that the 3090 cannot reproduce, because card 0 boots it on the pinned path over-committed. So the two cards split the answer and neither could give both: card 0 has the acceptance at width 15, the 3090 has the deployability.
- **On this host an over-committed KV pin does not fail, it runs three to five times slower.** The boots of the block-16 drafter that served at 146 to 164 ms per step were the ones whose pinned KV cache, beside the 4 GiB drafter and the graph reservation, exceeded the card (measured on both cards by the adapter counters: shared usage 1.8 to 1.9 GB and total committed 25.2 to 25.3 GB on a 24.6 GB card, against a shared baseline of 86 MB; the width-11 and width-15 acceptance cells ran in that state, which does not touch acceptance); on the native 3090 the same configuration is a hard OOM at allocation, on WSL2 it runs, because GPU allocations beyond the card spill into host memory. At a pin that fits, with the adapter counters showing both working sets on the card, the same drafter with the fork's verify kernel on serves at 49.4 ms per step against the DFlash2 head's 23.3 at the same pin and context (2.1 times), where the 3090's matched pair reads 30.9 against 25.6 (1.2 times); that residual, about 26 ms per step here against 5 there, is a real cost of the plain-DFlash path on this host with the over-commit excluded, mechanism unknown. So a WSL2 user gets a silently slow server where a native user gets an error, and the memory item and this one are the same shortfall reported two ways. The arc-two family of host properties keeps its two members (the width-15 kernel cost, the slow-boot state) and this is not a third.
- **A published Qwen3 DSpark drafter cannot be served by vLLM 0.28 as published.** Two independent upstream defects hit before it loads, on both boxes at the same lines: the loader asks for the draft's quantization through the target's composed overrides and refuses them, and the model registry routes the checkpoint's architecture name to the DeepSeek V4 class. One line and one renamed config get past them; neither error names the cause.
- **The one illegal memory access** (the block-16 drafter with the fork's verify kernel on, card 0, the over-committed pin) did not reproduce once the pin fit: the same boot with the kernel on served. That was the memory state, not the kernel.
- **Lookup drafting at width 7** is worth between about minus 4 and plus 2 percent of accepted tokens on this cohort at unchanged step cost, the two boxes disagreeing on the sign inside that band (one boot per arm each). Not a finding; a sentence beside item 6 of the note.

## On training a drafter, from the research

The record's answer stands: the DFlash2 head is five full-width layers (about 1.2 GB at 4-bit), DFlash's recipe is public and DFlash2's is not, SpecForge trains both with a two-GPU reference topology, two 4090s can do a warm-started fine-tune against the W4A16 target (3 to 15 dollars a run rented) and cannot train from scratch, and the community block-16 drafter that this arc measured was the free version of the "wider block" question. What this arc adds: a wider block is worth about one percent of throughput at best on this cohort even where it is free, so the fine-tune worth paying for is the one aimed at positions 0 to 6 (the quantized-target hidden-state hypothesis), not at width.
