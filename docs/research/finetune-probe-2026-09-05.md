## Ten-line summary (as delivered)

1. Yes. SpecForge's DFlash2 trainer ran optimizer steps on this box's RTX 4090 (host GPU 1), warm-started from the shipped W4A16 head (dequantized to bf16, 81/81 tensors, 1,924,404,480 params), against hidden states captured from the W4A16 target in vLLM.
2. Warm-start sanity check passed: on the first batch the untouched head scored position-1 top-1 accuracy 0.644 and selector serving accepted length 3.20, in line with the repo's measured acceptance, so the layer convention (target id i = output of 0-indexed layer i, which SpecForge and vLLM share) and the dequantization are right.
3. The recipe's 512 anchors do not fit 24 GiB: those runs took 115 to 129 s per step because under WSL2 CUDA allocations silently spill into host RAM instead of raising OOM (the optimizer benchmark reported a 43 GiB peak on a 24 GiB card).
4. At 256 anchors the same steps take 6.3 to 7.6 s (peak 23.3 GiB as seen by nvidia-smi); at 128 anchors 6.5 to 7.1 s (20.6 GiB). The floor is the CPU-offloaded AdamW step, 6.5 s per optimizer step for 1.92B params.
5. With gradient accumulation 8 the amortized cost is 1.26 s per sample (10.05 s per optimizer step over 8 samples of about 617 tokens); forward+backward is about 0.45 s per sample.
6. Estimate for a 50k-sample warm-start epoch on one 4090: about 17.5 h of training (9 h at accumulation 32) plus 15 to 25 h of on-policy generation and capture, so 33 to 43 GPU-hours; each extra epoch adds 9 to 18 h. Hidden states are 30.8 MB per sample (1.5 TB for 50k), so capture must be chunked and rotated.
7. Three environment failures had to be cleared, none in SpecForge itself: expandable_segments:True breaks Marlin repack under WSL2 (the repo's launcher documents this), flashinfer's JIT top-k in the DFlash2 speculator needed ninja on PATH, and Docker's --gpus device=1 is not honoured on this host (the container saw both cards; pinned by UUID).
8. Two SpecForge quirks matter for a real run: under the single-rank DDP path the trainer reads the selector weight off the DDP wrapper and gets 0, so the selector term was silently off in every probe step; and its flex-attention wrapper compiles with dynamic shapes off, which invites per-shape recompiles (measured below the ceiling only with sdpa).
9. Every run also wrote a 26 GB end-of-run checkpoint (fp32 masters and Adam moments included) even with save_interval: 0, taking about 2.5 min over the 9P mount.
10. Riskiest remaining unknown: the round trip back to serving. Nothing here exported a trained head, loaded it in the fork's vLLM, or measured acceptance, and the rwmacy card's position-offset trap sits exactly on that path.
