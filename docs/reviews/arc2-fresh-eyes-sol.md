## 3. Determinism

The defensible statement is observational: the sampled launcher default replayed in all boots tested. The doc says five; mine is 32 of 32 across those five, and the four later `r7` boots make nine matching boots in the current raw directory. It is not a proof of determinism outside this cohort, seed set, cache state and configuration.

"Exactly two groups" is established only as the count observed among five card-0 width-15 boots. Mine finds `{std1, std1b, lk15off}` identical within group, `{std2, c0cc0}` identical within group, and 22 of 32 rows identical across groups, exactly the doc's ten-row separation. Five draws cannot establish that a third group has zero probability. "Consistent with a binary state" is warranted; "the state is binary" is not.

The card-1 forced trio really contains three distinct trajectory vectors: mine is 24 of 32, 31 of 32 and 23 of 32 pairwise, matching the doc. All three produce round 15.000 and the policy source returns the same full block whenever the threshold comparison is true (`patches/dflash2-lookup-drafting.patch:395-408`). Even so, the thresholds are three different nominal configurations with only one boot each. Thus they falsify a one-bit model pooled across same-realized-behaviour settings, but they do not falsify a binary state within any one exact configuration. The preregistered `pc0c` and `pc0d` same-configuration group count is the clean test.

The native "prefix caching hides it" story does not stand. The image couples `--enable-prefix-caching` and `--mamba-cache-mode align` in one conditional (`6869c80:single-user/start_qwen.sh:442-450`). The original native cache-off arm removed both. At width 7, the later one-variable arm reportedly replayed with caching off and align kept, while removing align split it. That assigns the observed native default result to align, not prefix caching. There is no one-variable native width-15 cache-off result in this tree, so the write-up must not say the width-15 state was hidden by prefix caching.

Finally, "removing X increases divergence" rules out X as the sole necessary source, not as a source or contributor. Mine confirms force-first 6 of 32 identical versus the ordinary same-configuration 29 of 32, and cache-off 17 of 32 versus 29 of 32. Force-first fixes only the configurations controlled by that flag and may make another numerical perturbation more visible. Cache-off changes the computation and trajectory regime and may likewise expose or interact with another perturbation. The correct inference is "neither intervention removes divergence, so another source exists". The write-up's categorical exclusion of the Triton race and prefix caching is stronger than the experiments.

## 5. Force-first-config classification

The right present classification is **a caution with a defect still unclassified**, not the stronger "not a defect". Mine confirms two one-token rows in each sampled card-0 forced boot, prompt 5 seeds 2 and 3 (`../flightbench/results/raw/v028/ffc0-ff7a.txt:45,59`; `../flightbench/results/raw/v028/ffc0-ff7b.txt:45,59`), and no one-token row in the local greedy forced boot. The native results are reported as the same sampled pair and clean greedy. The two sampled boots also run doc/mine 46.9/46.93 and 45.0/45.08 ms, about twice the default 23.2, and replay only doc/mine 4 of 30/4 of 30 after excluding the two shared empty rows.

The registered greedy classifier in `../flightbench/results/raw/v028/.ffg.sh:2-9` is not a sound defect boundary. A broken numerical kernel can move EOS from negligible probability into top-k without making it top-1. Greedy only establishes that EOS is not the argmax. Conversely, one seeded EOS sample can be legitimate even if the distributions differ harmlessly. The fact that `sa0_15` also emits one token on prompt 5 seed 2 under a different kernel switch (`../flightbench/results/raw/v028/lkcost-sa0_15.txt:150`) shows that this prompt is a sensitive boundary and weakens a flag-specific correctness story.

To call it a defect I would require a first-token logit A/B for prompt 5 under forced and autotuned configurations against a trusted implementation, including EOS rank and probability; a repeated-seed distributional test showing excess EOS frequency beyond sampling error; and a kernel-level numerical comparison that identifies which forced configuration exceeds an agreed tolerance. A reproducible greedy argmax flip would be sufficient evidence of a severe case, but it is not necessary for a sampled-distribution defect.
