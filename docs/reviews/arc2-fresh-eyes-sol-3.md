## 2. B: the cold pair

The row arithmetic is sound: `cold7a` is 27.98 ms per steady step and `cold7b` 33.80, a 5.82 ms difference. Both have 32 active rows, round 7.000, and the same cache-disable configuration. `analyze_winners.py` finds six winners and four out-of-resource failures in each. Their winner sets differ on four of six kernels, exactly as the record says.

Clause (i) is only partly supported. The four graph-captured races named in the question, `cold7a`, `cold7b`, `t11`, and `sa0_7a`, do have four distinct six-kernel winner sets. But the first-listed counts produced by the requested analyzer are 1/6, 1/6, **0/6**, and 1/6 respectively. The raw `t11` lines confirm the zero: for example its first blockdim64 candidate is BV 32 and its winner is BV 64. Thus "one of six each time" is false for `t11`. The older three-race statements at validation lines 1424 and 1426 need the same correction if `t11` is among their three.

Clause (ii) is supported as an observation, not an attribution. The two flag-on boots differ by 5.8 ms and their winner set is the only captured configuration difference. That does not prove that winners cause the whole difference. Compile-duration variation, unlogged process state, and timing noise remain possible. Validation line 1430 crosses that boundary when it says the winner set "moves the step cost by at least that much" and that a boot's speed is set by the race. The human write-up and note do not repeat that stronger causal claim.

Clause (iii) is supported. Fresh compile plus a race is not by itself a 28 to 34 ms condition: `t11` and `sa0_7a` compiled and raced with the cache enabled at 23.43 and 23.61 ms. The fixed cost of `VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE=1` remains unresolved. The proposed normal-cache boot with `cold7a`'s six winners made first is a sensible discriminator and is registered in validation lines 1426 and 1430, but no runnable pin cell or result exists. The outgoing drafts make nothing stronger about the cold pair.
